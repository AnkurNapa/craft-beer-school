Nonic pintAmerican Amber Lager

The lager for people who want a bit more bread in the glass, without giving up the clean finish.

Written byAnkur NapaMaster Brewer and course instructor
Written byChatty GirijaBeer podcaster and creative strategist

By Ankur Napa and Chatty Girija

Colour6-14 SRM
Bitterness18-30 IBU
Strength4.8-5.4%

Serve in a nonic pint at 5-8 °C

TasteProfileIn IndiaFoodBrewed here

What it tastes like

Think of a standard lager, then add a slice of toast. That is the amber lager in a sentence. Brewers take a clean lager base and lean on crystal and Munich-type malts for colour and a gentle caramel note, then keep the hops polite so the malt can lead. It finishes drier than its colour suggests, because a cold, slow lager fermentation strips out the fruity esters an ale would leave behind.

The style grew up in American brewpubs and regional breweries in the 1980s and 1990s, as a step up from pale mass-market lager that still felt familiar. It borrows from the Vienna and Märzen tradition but is looser about it: some versions carry a light citrus or floral hop note from American varieties, others stay strictly malty. What keeps it honest is balance. A good one is easy to drink by the pint, with enough malt to stand up to food and enough bitterness that the second glass does not feel sweet.

Style profile

Look
Copper to light reddish brown, bright and clear after lagering, with an off-white head that holds well.
Aroma and flavour
Toast, light caramel and biscuit up front, a little hop spice or citrus behind it, no fruity yeast character.
Mouthfeel
Medium-light body, lively carbonation and a clean, fairly dry finish with a soft bitter edge.
Ingredients
Pale base malt with crystal and Munich or Vienna malt, American or noble-type hops, a clean lager yeast, moderate water hardness.

In India

Amber lager suits Indian drinkers who find stouts too heavy but want more than a fizzy pale lager. It travels better than hop-forward beers because its appeal is malt, which fades more slowly in a warm warehouse than aroma hops do. You will mostly find it on tap at brewpubs that run a German or American house list, especially in Bengaluru. Serve it cold but not frozen; at near zero the toast disappears. It works well at a long weekend lunch or a monsoon evening with fried snacks.

At the Indian table

Food pairings contributed by Anuradha Rao

  • Tandoori chickenThe char on the chicken meets the toasted malt halfway, and the clean finish resets the palate between pieces.
  • Paneer tikkaSmoky edges on the paneer echo the caramel malt while the carbonation lifts the richness of the cheese.
  • Onion bhaji with green chutneyLight bitterness and fizz cut through the fried batter without burying the onion.
  • Gajar ka halwaCaramel notes in the beer pick up the cooked carrot and ghee, and the dry finish stops it cloying.
