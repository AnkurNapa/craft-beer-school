Setting up a Google Business Profile that brings diners to a brewpub

When someone in Bengaluru searches "brewery near me", the first thing they see is a map and a handful of Google Business Profiles. Here is how to set yours up so it brings in diners, not just views.

3 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

Why this profile matters so much

For a brewpub, a Google Business Profile is often the first impression. People search on Maps for somewhere to go tonight, compare photos and ratings, check the hours and tap for directions. Many decide without ever visiting your website or Instagram. Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence, so a complete, active profile with good reviews gives you the best chance of showing up.

It is also free, which makes a neglected profile one of the most expensive mistakes a new brewpub can make.

Setting it up properly

  1. Claim or create the profile with the exact business name on your signage. Do not stuff keywords into the name, which breaks Google's rules and can get the profile suspended.
  2. Pick the right primary category. "Brewpub" or "Brewery" is usually best, with secondary categories such as "Bar" or "Restaurant" if they genuinely apply.
  3. Verify the business. Google offers several methods, including video verification. Follow the steps it gives you and keep your signage visible in any video.
  4. Set the exact address and map pin. In Indian malls and tech parks, the pin often lands at the wrong gate. Drag it to the entrance guests should use.
  5. Add hours, including special hours for dry days and holidays.

That last point matters in India. Dry days vary by state and are announced at short notice for elections and festivals. Update your hours each time, or guests arrive to a closed bar and leave a one-star review.

Photos that sell the room

Photos drive clicks more than almost anything else on the profile. Add bright, real images of the room, the brewhouse, the bar, the food and the beer. Show the space at the busy, lively time of day you want to sell. Replace dark or blurry photos uploaded by guests by adding many better ones of your own. Our guide to photographing beer covers getting a good shot with a phone.

Keep photos responsible: no scenes of heavy drinking and nothing that looks aimed at minors.

Menu, attributes and posts

Add your food and beer menu, or link to it. Keep it up to date. Fill in attributes such as outdoor seating, live music, wheelchair access, parking and whether you take bookings. These help Google match you to searches.

Use posts for events and new beers. A short post with a good photo for a quiz night or a new release keeps the profile looking active.

Reviews: ask, read, reply

Reviews affect both ranking and choice. Ask happy guests to leave one, with a QR code on the bill folder or table tent. Never pay for reviews, offer free beer in exchange or post fake ones. That breaks Google's rules and Indian consumer rules on fake reviews.

Reply to every review, good or bad, in your brand voice. Thank people who praise a specific beer. For complaints, apologise for the experience, avoid arguing and offer to talk privately. Future guests read your replies as much as the reviews. Look for patterns too: three reviews about slow service on Fridays is a staffing problem, not a marketing one.

Your first month

If you are opening a brewpub, set the profile up before launch so it is verified and complete on day one. In the first month, ask early guests for reviews, add photos weekly and post every event. Our guide to planning a brewpub opening month covers the rest of the launch checklist.

After that, set a weekly routine: check new reviews and reply, add a few photos, post anything new and check the hours. Fifteen minutes a week keeps the profile working.

Reread the business description every few months. Guests often want to know about parking, children, pets and whether you serve food for people who do not drink. Answer those in the description and attributes, so nobody has to guess or rely on an old review for the answer.

Connect it to the rest of your marketing

A Google profile works best alongside a clear website, active social channels and a way to book a table. Link them all. Use the profile's insights to see how people find you and what they do next, such as calls, direction requests or website visits. That tells you which parts of your marketing bring people through the door, which is the only number that really matters for a brewpub.

Common questions

What category should a brewpub choose on Google?

Usually "Brewpub" or "Brewery" as the primary category, with "Bar" or "Restaurant" as secondary if they genuinely apply.

Can I offer free beer for Google reviews?

No. Incentivised or fake reviews break Google's policies and Indian consumer rules. Ask happy guests politely instead.

How do I handle dry days on my Google profile?

Add special hours for each dry day as soon as it is announced, so guests do not arrive to a closed bar.
