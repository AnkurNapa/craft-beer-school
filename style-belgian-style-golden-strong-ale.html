TulipBelgian Golden Strong Ale

Looks like a lager, drinks like champagne, and is often nearly as strong as wine. Respect it.

Written byAnkur NapaMaster Brewer and course instructor
Written byChatty GirijaBeer podcaster and creative strategist

By Ankur Napa and Chatty Girija

Colour3-10 SRM
Bitterness20-50 IBU
Strength7-11%

Serve in a tulip at 7-10 °C

TasteProfileIn IndiaFoodBrewed here

What it tastes like

The golden strong ale is a trick played on the eye. It looks pale and bright, like a pilsner, but it can be as strong as a light wine. The style took its modern shape around 1970, when Duvel Moortgat turned its strong ale pale to answer the fashion for golden lager: very light pilsner malt, a good dose of plain sugar to thin the body, and a vigorous Belgian yeast.

The sugar is the secret. It ferments away completely, so the beer finishes very dry and light despite all that alcohol. Pear, apple and a little citrus come from the yeast, along with a peppery spice. The hops are more noticeable than in a blonde, adding a fine bitterness and a herbal or spicy note that keeps the finish crisp. Carbonation is high, sometimes close to sparkling wine. It is less sweet than a tripel, though the two are close cousins and many drinkers confuse them.

Style profile

Look
Pale straw to deep gold, brilliantly clear, with a huge, rocky white head that lasts.
Aroma and flavour
Pear, apple and citrus from the yeast, peppery spice, a soft pale malt base and a clean herbal hop note.
Mouthfeel
Light body for its strength, very high carbonation, bone-dry and warming at the finish.
Ingredients
Pilsner malt, a large share of plain white sugar, Saaz or Styrian Golding type hops, a highly attenuative Belgian yeast.

In India

This is a beer to share, not a summer session drink. In Indian heat its lightness tempts you to drink it like lager, which at eight or nine per cent is a mistake. It makes a good alternative to sparkling wine at a wedding or anniversary, poured into tulips in small measures. Few Indian brewers make one, as it needs a strong, healthy yeast and careful fermentation control. Imported bottles turn up in premium shops; check the date and buy cold.
