Starting a brewery in a tier 2 Indian city and the demand to expect

Tier 2 cities can be good places for a brewpub, with lower costs and less competition. Demand behaves differently from Bengaluru or Pune, though. Here is how to think about who will come, what they will drink and how to size the brewery for it.

The appeal and the catch

The case for a tier 2 city writes itself. Rent per square foot is a fraction of a metro high street. Labour costs less. You may be the first brewpub in town, with local press and curiosity on your side. Our guide to choosing a brewery location covers the site side of that decision.

The catch is that lower costs usually come with lower prices and a smaller pool of regular craft beer drinkers. First-month curiosity is not repeat demand. The question that matters is how many people will come back on an ordinary Wednesday in the third month.

Check the licence before the market

Microbrewery licences exist only in states that have notified a policy for them. Conditions differ widely. Some states limit microbreweries to certain cities or set minimum premises sizes, production caps or rules on where the beer can be sold. Confirm that your city and your site qualify under the current state policy before you study demand at all. Rules change, so check the latest notification or ask a licensing consultant.

Who will actually come

In metros, a brewpub can survive on a core of beer enthusiasts and young professionals. In a smaller city the crowd is usually broader and more mixed:

  • Families and groups celebrating birthdays and anniversaries, where food matters as much as beer.
  • Local business owners and professionals looking for a good place to host.
  • Students and young workers, often price sensitive.
  • Visitors from the nearest metro, who come at weekends and on holidays.

Many of these guests will try craft beer for the first time with you. Some will not drink at all. A strong food menu, good non-alcoholic options and a family-friendly room are not extras here. They are the business.

What sells

Expect the familiar to sell. A clean, crisp lager, a wheat beer and perhaps a gentle fruit beer often carry most of the volume in a market new to craft beer. A bitter double IPA might win praise from a handful of regulars and still sit in the tank. Brew it, by all means, but in a small batch.

That shapes the brewery. A lager-heavy menu needs more fermenter time, because lagers spend weeks conditioning cold. A brewhouse that looks big enough on paper can be short of tank space if most of what you sell is lager.

Weekends carry the week

Brewpubs everywhere do more trade at weekends, but in smaller cities the gap is often sharper. Friday to Sunday can carry most of the week's revenue, with weekday evenings quiet outside festivals and wedding season. Seasons matter too: exam months, the monsoon and long festival periods all shift footfall.

Plan staffing and brewing around that shape. A brewer who packages on Thursday and a kitchen rota built for weekend peaks will save you more money than any discount campaign.

Estimating demand before you sign

There is no reliable published figure for craft beer demand in any one city. Anyone selling you one should be asked where it came from. Build your own estimate from local evidence:

  1. Visit the busiest restaurants and bars in town on a Wednesday and a Saturday. Count covers and note what people order.
  2. Ask liquor retailers which beers move and at what price points.
  3. Estimate your covers per day by day of the week, then the share of guests who order beer and the average volume per beer drinker.
  4. Multiply out to monthly litres, then halve your optimism for the first year.

Compare that volume with what your planned brewhouse costs, as set out in our guide to microbrewery costs in India. If the brewery only works at a full house every night, it does not work.

The talent question

One cost that does not fall in a smaller city is skilled people. Qualified brewers are scarce and often prefer metros. Budget for relocation, a competitive salary or a consultant brewer who visits regularly. Train local staff deeply, because they will be the ones explaining a wheat beer to a table that has only ever ordered a strong lager. For the bigger picture of where the market is heading, see our guide to craft beer market trends in India.

Common questions

Is a tier 2 city a good place for a brewpub?

It can be, with lower rent and less competition. Demand is usually smaller, more weekend-heavy and more food-led, so size the brewery and menu for that.

Which beer styles sell best in a new craft market?

Familiar, approachable styles such as a clean lager and a wheat beer usually carry most of the volume. Keep bolder styles to small batches at first.

How do I estimate brewpub demand in a smaller city?

Count covers at busy local restaurants and bars, talk to liquor retailers and build a day-by-day estimate of guests and beer volume. Then plan for less than your first estimate.
