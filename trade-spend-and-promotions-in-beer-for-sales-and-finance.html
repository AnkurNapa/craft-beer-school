Trade spend and promotions in beer, for sales and finance teams

Brewers spend heavily to get beer stocked, displayed, chilled and pushed by the people who sell it. Sales sees that spend as growth. Finance sees it as leakage. This guide gives both teams a shared way to understand it.

3 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

What counts as trade spend

Trade spend is money a brewer spends on the trade, the outlets and distributors between brewery and drinker, rather than directly on consumers. In beer it typically covers:

  • Schemes and incentives to retailers and bars for volume or listing.
  • Visibility: shelf space, branding at the outlet, menus, glassware and signage where rules allow.
  • Coolers and draught equipment placed in outlets.
  • Support for events, launches and activations in on-trade venues.
  • Distributor margins and incentives in states with private wholesale.

Because alcohol advertising is restricted in India, a good deal of brand building happens at the outlet. That pushes trade spend up compared with categories that can advertise freely.

The rules shape what is possible

State excise rules control a lot of what a brewer can offer. Many states fix or approve the maximum retail price, so discounts to the consumer are limited or not allowed. Some restrict promotional schemes, free goods or certain incentives altogether. In states where a corporation is the wholesaler, the brewer may have no direct commercial relationship with the retailer at all.

The point for finance and sales teams is simple: before designing or approving any promotion, check what the current rules in that state allow with your legal and compliance colleagues. What is normal in one state can be prohibited in the next. Our guide to beer pricing strategy covers the pricing side.

On-trade and off-trade spend differently

On-trade venues, bars, restaurants and brewpubs, sell an experience. Spend there buys draught taps, menu placement, staff training and events. Off-trade shops sell packs to take home. Spend there buys space, visibility and chilled stock. The returns behave differently. A tap installed in a busy bar can sell for years. A shop scheme may lift volume for a month and then fade. See on-trade versus off-trade for more on the channels.

The calendar adds pressure. Summer and the festive season are when outlets expect support, so most spend lands in a few months. That is also when stock is tight and supply teams are stretched. A scheme that drives volume the brewery cannot supply wastes money twice: once on the scheme and again on disappointed outlets. Agree promotion plans with supply planning before they are announced to the trade.

Accounting for it properly

Trade spend is easy to lose track of because it is paid in many small amounts, often after the event, against claims from the trade. A few principles keep it under control:

  • Accrue as you go. Book the expected cost of a scheme in the period the volume is sold, not when the claim arrives months later.
  • Classify consistently. Decide which items reduce revenue and which are expenses, following your accounting policy. Mixed treatment makes net revenue per litre meaningless across periods.
  • Tie spend to outlets and brands. Spend that cannot be traced to an outlet or brand cannot be evaluated.
  • Treat equipment as assets. Coolers and taps have a life, a cost and a location. Track them like assets, because they go missing like assets.

Did it pay back?

The honest answer is often unknown, because the comparison is missing. Good post-event evaluation compares outlets that got the spend with similar outlets that did not, over the same weeks. It adjusts for season. It asks whether volume was gained or merely pulled forward from next month. It also asks whether the lift came from your other brands, which would mean the business paid to move drinkers between its own beers.

Brand portfolios add a layer here: a scheme on a strong beer can cannibalise a mild one in the same family. Our guide to beer brand portfolios explains how brands interact.

Finance and sales usually agree on the maths once they agree on the comparison. The harder part is knowing what is realistic: how fast a cooler pays for itself in a hot month, how long draught lines last, what a fresher stock rotation does to repeat sales. That knowledge sits on the brewery and market floor rather than in the ledger.

A short checklist before approving spend

  1. Is it allowed under the current rules in this state?
  2. Which outlets, brands and packs does it cover?
  3. What is the comparison group and the measurement window?
  4. How will claims be verified?
  5. What will we stop doing if it does not work?

Common questions

Why is trade spend so high in Indian beer?

Because alcohol advertising is restricted, much brand building happens at the outlet through visibility, coolers and on-trade support.

Can brewers offer consumer discounts in India?

It depends on the state. Many states fix or approve retail prices and restrict schemes. Always check current rules with compliance colleagues.

How do you measure the return on a trade scheme?

Compare outlets that received the spend with similar outlets that did not, over the same period. Then check whether volume was gained or simply pulled forward.
