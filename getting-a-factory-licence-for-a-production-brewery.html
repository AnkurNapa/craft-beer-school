Getting a factory licence for a production brewery

A brewpub can sometimes open under a bar licence and a brewing permission. A production brewery that packages beer for sale sits in a different box. Here is how the factory licence fits among the other approvals.

3 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

Why a production brewery is a factory

A brewery that makes beer for sale and packages it in cans, bottles or kegs is a manufacturing unit. State laws treat manufacturing premises as factories once they pass certain thresholds of workers and use of power. Because thresholds and forms change, ask the factories department of your state or a licensing adviser what applies to your size.

The factory licence is one approval in a chain. It sits next to the excise licence to manufacture, the building approvals and the pollution control consents. Our guide on the microbrewery licence in India shows the excise side.

The sequence that saves time

Approvals depend on each other. A typical order, which varies by state, looks like this:

  1. Company registration and a valid lease or title for an industrial or permitted zone.
  2. Land use and building plan approval.
  3. Fire safety approval for the building.
  4. Pollution control board consent to establish, before you build the plant.
  5. Factory plan approval and then the factory licence.
  6. Consent to operate, once the plant is ready.
  7. Excise licence to manufacture, with the plant inspected.

Do not assume this is your state's order. Ask each office what it needs from the other. Many states now run a single window portal for industrial approvals, which can cut the shuttling between offices.

Zoning comes first

A brewery can be refused for a site where it cannot legally operate. Industrial areas are usually the safest. Commercial or mixed-use areas may allow small breweries with conditions. Ask the planning authority for the permitted use of your plot in writing before you sign a lease or pay a deposit. Our guide on getting groundwater permission for a brewery borewell covers the water side of the same site decision.

What the factory inspector looks at

The factory plan approval and licence are about safety and working conditions. Expect questions on the layout, ventilation, machine guarding, emergency exits, drinking water and toilets for workers. Electrical safety is checked too. A brewery adds its own hazards: carbon dioxide in cellars and fermentation rooms, caustic and acid in the cleaning-in-place system, hot liquor and wort, pressure vessels and forklifts moving kegs.

If you run a steam boiler, expect a boiler inspection and certificate under the boiler rules. Electric heating on a small plant avoids that requirement, but check your state. Carbon dioxide detectors in the fermentation hall and a posted procedure for entering tanks tell an inspector you take safety seriously.

Pollution control and water

A brewery produces effluent: spent grain water, rinse water, caustic and acid streams and yeast waste. Pollution control boards want to know the volume and how you treat it. A plant with its own effluent treatment plant and a clear water balance gets through consent faster than one that leaves the question open. Spent grain is a product with value. Local dairy farms often collect it. A written arrangement shows the board where it goes.

Documents to keep ready

  • Lease or title papers and the zoning confirmation.
  • Building plan approval and the fire approval.
  • Factory layout with equipment positions and exits.
  • List of equipment, boiler and pressure vessels.
  • Water source papers and the effluent plan.
  • Worker count, shifts and safety training records.

Keep these in one binder and one scanned folder. Every office asks for the same papers in a different order.

Where founders lose time

The common delays are all in sequencing. Equipment is ordered before the consent to establish arrives. A lease is signed before zoning is confirmed. An inspection is booked before the fire approval is in hand. Write the sequence on one page with the office, the document and the realistic wait for each step. Then line it up with the equipment delivery date. A licensing adviser who knows your state earns their fee here.

One more habit helps. Ask every office for its checklist in writing and keep the date you asked. When two offices give different answers, the dated list lets you go back and ask which one applies. Most delays in this chain come from guessing at what an office wants rather than asking for it.

Common questions

Does a production brewery need a factory licence?

Often yes, depending on the number of workers, the use of power and your state's rules. Ask your state's factories department or a licensing adviser.

Which approval comes first for a new brewery?

Zoning and a valid lease come first. Pollution control consent to establish usually comes before construction. The order varies by state.

Do I need a boiler certificate?

If you run a steam boiler, usually yes, with an inspection under the boiler rules. Electric heating avoids the boiler but check your state's rules.

Can a licensing adviser speed this up?

An adviser who knows your state can help you sequence the approvals and prepare papers correctly. They cannot promise a licence. Approvals depend on the authorities.
