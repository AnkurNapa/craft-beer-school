The 2026 hop harvest: supply, prices and what it means for brewers

Hops went into 2026 in surplus. Then Europe baked. We pull together the BarthHaas, USDA and Brewers Association figures with their dates. Then we explain what an Indian brewer buying imported hops should do this season.

4 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

The short version

The world still grows more hops than brewers use, but the surplus is uneven. American aroma and high-alpha hops look plentiful. German, Czech and Slovenian aroma hops, the noble types lager brewers rely on, came in short and low in alpha after a hot, dry summer. Your buying plan should follow that split.

Where 2025 left the market

The BarthHaas Report 2025/2026 put world hop area at 52,660 ha in 2025, down 5.5%. The crop came to 109,188 tonnes. Alpha output was 11,532 tonnes against demand of about 10,700 tonnes. That left a surplus of about 1,200 tonnes of alpha, or roughly 5,100 ha too many. BarthHaas expected another surplus of about 1,000 tonnes, with world beer output down 1% to just under 1.9 billion hectolitres.

Acreage in 2026

Growers kept cutting. The BarthHaas June 2026 update estimated world area at about 51,350 ha, down roughly 3%. Germany fell 5.8% to 17,861 ha. The US Pacific Northwest held at 16,853 ha, with Citra up 6% to about 19% of the area and CTZ down 19%.

In acres, the USDA June figures reported by the Brewers Association on 14 July came to 41,642 acres strung for harvest, barely changed on 2025.

A hot summer in Europe

The official German estimate, published in the BarthHaas update of 31 August, is 33,779 tonnes, 22% below 2025's 43,141 tonnes and about 17% below an average year. Early analyses show exceptionally low alpha. The Brewers Association noted on 22 September that Kitzingen hit a Bavarian record of 40.8 °C, rainfall ran 34% below average and less than 20% of German hop land is irrigated. Perle, Tettnanger and Hallertau Mittelfrüh suffered most.

Czech Saaz showed a historically low 1.8% alpha. Slovenia lost about 900 ha to hail. The US crop, by contrast, was rated average to slightly above.

Contracts, the alpha clause and stocks

BarthHaas expects significant shortfalls on forward contracts from Germany, the Czech Republic and Slovenia. Aroma contracts sold per kilogram of alpha may not be filled in full. The alpha clause usually applies when a variety falls at least 15% below its ten-year average, with decisions due in mid-October.

The US side has slack. USDA stocks released on 18 September show 105 million pounds of hops held on 1 September 2026, down 9% from 116 million a year earlier, with 86 million held by dealers and growers. No merchant has published 2026 spot prices, so treat any figure you hear as a quote, not a market rate. Our reading is that spot noble hops will be tight while US spot stays easy.

Why low alpha costs you money

Alpha acid is what turns into bitterness in the boil. When alpha drops, a brewer needs more kilograms of hop for the same IBU, so the same beer costs more to bitter. On a 10 hl brew of a 30 IBU Pilsner, a hop that arrives at 3% alpha instead of 4% needs about a third more weight in the kettle. Aroma hops sold per kilogram of alpha protect the brewer on paper, but only if the merchant has the alpha to deliver. That is why the contract shortfalls matter even to brewers who never signed one: whatever is left over for spot buyers is the part nobody contracted.

What an Indian brewer importing hops should do

Most craft brewers here buy on spot through importers, a few bags at a time. This season:

  • Lager hops first. If your house Pilsner runs on Mittelfrüh, Tettnanger or Saaz, place the order now and ask whether stock is 2025 or 2026 crop.
  • Recalculate bitterness. Use the alpha on the certificate of analysis, not last year's. A 2026 Saaz at 1.8% gives far fewer IBUs than the same weight from a normal year.
  • Have a substitute. Hüll Classic, Titan and Tango held up better in the heat. See our new hop varieties guide.
  • Buy only what you can freeze. A chest freezer at around -18 °C keeps vacuum packs well. A warm godown ruins the bargain. Our hop storage guide covers this.

For the paperwork side, see sourcing malt and hops in India.

What to watch next

Three dates matter. The alpha clause decisions in mid-October, the USDA National Hop Report due on 18 December and the BarthHaas Report on the 2026 crop, usually out in the summer after harvest. If German alpha comes in as low as feared, expect merchants to push concentrated products and US aroma hops harder in 2027.

Common questions

Is there a hop shortage in 2026?

Not overall. The world still has surplus hops, mostly American. German, Czech and Slovenian aroma hops are short and low in alpha after a hot, dry summer.

Why does low alpha matter to a small brewer?

Bitterness is calculated from alpha. If you dose by last year's figure, a low-alpha 2026 hop will give a less bitter beer than you planned.

Should an Indian brewery sign a hop contract?

If you brew the same beer all year with the same hop, a contract through your importer protects supply. For occasional beers, spot buying is usually fine in a surplus market.
