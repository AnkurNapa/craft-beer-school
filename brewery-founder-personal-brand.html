Building a founder personal brand alongside the brewery brand

Many of the strongest craft beer brands have a visible founder behind them. Here is how to build a personal brand that helps your brewery grow and how to keep the two in balance.

3 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer
Point of contactChatty GirijaQuestions about this guide? WhatsApp her

  1. Why founders matter in craft beer
  2. Decide what you want to be known for
  3. Choose your channels
  4. What to share

Why founders matter in craft beer

Craft beer is personal. Drinkers like to know who makes the beer, why they started and what they care about. In India, where alcohol advertising is restricted, a founder's own voice can be one of the few ways to tell the brewery's story. It also helps with things the brewery logo cannot do on its own: talking to investors, hiring brewers, building relationships with distributors and landing media coverage.

A personal brand here simply means being known for something useful and consistent, so that people trust the brewery because they trust the person behind it. You do not need a large following. Fifty of the right people in distribution, hospitality and the press are worth more than ten thousand strangers.

Decide what you want to be known for

Pick one or two themes that match both your real expertise and the brewery's position. A founder who was a brewer might be known for brewing with Indian ingredients. A founder from finance might share what it takes to run a brewery as a business. Someone from hospitality might talk about taproom culture.

The theme should support the brewery brand, not compete with it. Our guide to beer brand storytelling helps you find the real story that both brands can share.

Choose your channels

LinkedIn suits founders who want to reach investors, partners, corporate buyers and future hires. Posts about lessons from building the business, the industry and hiring often do well. Keep product promotion light.

Instagram suits founders who want to reach drinkers, with behind-the-scenes posts from the brewery, travel and the people who make the beer.

Talks, podcasts and festivals build credibility offline. A founder who speaks well about brewing in India becomes a go-to name for journalists.

Pick one main channel and do it well rather than spreading thin across all of them.

What to share

  • Honest lessons from running a brewery, including mistakes.
  • The people behind the beer: brewers, staff, suppliers.
  • What you are learning about ingredients, styles or the industry.
  • Your view on where craft beer in India is going. Our guide to craft beer in India is useful background.

Avoid constant sales posts. If every post is "come drink our new beer", people stop listening. A good rule is that most posts should be useful or interesting even to someone who never visits the brewery.

Stay within the rules

A founder's personal account is not exempt from alcohol advertising rules. Posts that look like ads for beer, show heavy drinking or appeal to younger audiences carry the same risks as brewery posts. Talking about the business, the craft and the people is generally safer than promoting drinking. When in doubt, ask an adviser. Our guide to alcohol advertising rules in India gives the background.

Keep the balance right

A founder brand can grow so strong that the brewery depends on one person. That becomes a problem if the founder steps back, sells or simply has a bad week online. Share the spotlight with your head brewer and team. Let the brewery's own channels tell its story too. Make sure the brand voice and values are written down, so the brewery can stand on its own.

Also think about what you share. Strong political or personal opinions will attach to the brewery whether you like it or not. Decide in advance where your lines are.

A simple routine

Founders are busy. A personal brand built on one post a week, written in thirty minutes, will do more than an ambitious plan that stops after a month. Keep a running note of ideas from your week: a problem solved, a conversation with a supplier, a beer that surprised you. Turn one into a post each week. Reply to comments. After six months, you will have a body of work that people recognise.

Measure it loosely. Note when a distributor, journalist or job applicant mentions a post. Those mentions are the real return. They rarely show up in a follower count.

Fitting that personal voice into the brewery's wider digital marketing, so both work together, is where a plan helps more than effort alone.

Common questions

Should a brewery founder be active on social media?

It helps, especially in India where alcohol advertising is restricted. A consistent, honest founder voice builds trust in the brewery.

LinkedIn or Instagram for a brewery founder?

LinkedIn for investors, partners and hiring. Instagram for drinkers. Pick one main channel and do it well.

Do alcohol advertising rules apply to a founder's personal posts?

Posts that promote drinking carry the same risks whoever posts them. Talking about the business and the craft is generally safer.
