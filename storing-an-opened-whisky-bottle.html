Storing an opened whisky bottle: oxidation and how long it keeps

Whisky is far more stable than wine or beer once opened. It does slowly lose aroma and change character, though. An Indian summer speeds that up. A few simple habits keep a bottle tasting right to the last pour.

Whisky does not spoil, but it does change

At 40% ABV or more, whisky is too strong for spoilage microbes. An opened bottle will not go off the way an opened bottle of wine or beer does. It is safe to drink years later.

What changes is the flavour. Once opened, the bottle holds air as well as whisky. Oxygen slowly reacts with some aroma compounds and the most volatile ones escape every time you pull the cork. Over months the aroma can fade, the fruit can dull and the whisky can taste flatter. Some whiskies soften pleasantly at first. Most lose something in the long run.

Headspace is the main factor

The more air in the bottle, the faster the change. A bottle that is nine tenths full has very little air above the whisky and keeps well for a long time. A bottle with a quarter left has a lot of air for a small amount of whisky, so oxidation works faster on what remains.

As a rough guide, many whisky drinkers find a bottle keeps well for a year or two while it is more than half full. Once it is down to the last third, try to finish it within a few months, or move it into a smaller bottle. Treat these as guides rather than rules. Delicate, fruity whiskies fade faster than big sherried or peated ones.

Decanting into a smaller bottle

Moving the last of a bottle into a smaller, clean glass bottle that it nearly fills cuts the headspace and slows the change. Use a bottle with a good seal. Sample bottles of 50 to 200 ml with screw caps work well. Label it with the whisky's name and the date.

Rinse the small bottle with a little of the whisky before filling, so no water or detergent is left behind. Avoid crystal decanters for long storage. Some older lead crystal can leach lead into spirit over time. Many decanters do not seal tightly.

Heat and light

Heat speeds up every chemical change in whisky and pushes volatile aroma out of the bottle faster. Store bottles in the coolest dark place in your home, away from outer walls, kitchens, windows and lofts. In a Mumbai or Chennai summer an inside cupboard can be several degrees cooler than a shelf near a sunny window.

Light, especially sunlight, can fade colour and dull aroma, which is why many whiskies come in tubes or boxes. Keep the box. A fridge is not needed and very cold whisky smells of very little when you pour it.

Upright, with the cork in good order

Store whisky upright. Unlike wine, whisky should not be kept on its side, because high strength spirit can break down a cork over time and taint the whisky. Keep the cork dry from the inside and tight.

Corks dry out and crumble, especially in dry heat. If a cork crumbles, strain the whisky through a clean coffee filter and close the bottle with a spare stopper. Some collectors wrap the neck of a slow-drinking bottle in plastic film for extra protection, though a good stopper usually does the job.

Tasting a bottle over time

One simple way to learn how a whisky changes is to note it when you first open it, then again every few months. Write down the main aromas and how strong they feel. Our guide to whisky tasting for beginners gives a structure for this.

You will notice that some whiskies seem to open up after a few weeks of air, then slowly fade. Learning to spot early fading teaches you to describe aroma precisely, which is the core of sensory training.

Whisky does not age in the bottle

Unlike some wines, whisky stops maturing once it is bottled. A 12 year old whisky bottled today is still a 12 year old whisky in ten years. Sealed bottles change very slowly if stored well. If you want to keep a bottle for years, keep it sealed, upright and cool. Open it when you want to drink it. Our guide to storing beer at home shows how differently beer behaves, since beer is far more fragile.

Common questions

How long does an opened bottle of whisky last?

It stays safe indefinitely. Flavour usually holds well for a year or two while the bottle is more than half full, then fades faster as air replaces whisky.

Should I keep whisky in the fridge?

No. A cool, dark cupboard is better. Cold whisky gives off little aroma and the fridge offers no real benefit for spirit.

Does whisky get better after opening?

Some whiskies soften and open up for a few weeks after opening. Over longer periods most lose aroma, especially with a lot of air in the bottle.
