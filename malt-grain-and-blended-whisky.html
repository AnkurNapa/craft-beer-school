Malt, grain and blended whisky explained

Three words carry most of the meaning on a whisky label. Here is what each one means in production terms, using the Scotch definitions most of the world borrows.

3 min read · Updated July 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

Three words that do most of the work

Walk into any whisky meeting and you will hear malt, grain and blend within the first five minutes. The terms were defined most tightly in Scotland. Other countries borrowed them, sometimes with different rules underneath. We use the Scotch meanings here, then point out where other countries differ.

Malt whisky

Malt whisky is made only from malted barley, water and yeast. It is distilled in copper pot stills. A single malt is malt whisky from one distillery. The word single refers to the distillery, not to the cask. A typical single malt is a mix of dozens or hundreds of casks, all made at the same site.

Pot stills run in batches and leave more of the heavier flavour compounds in the spirit. That is why malts vary so much from one distillery to the next, even when the ingredients look identical on paper. Still shape, fermentation time, cut points and casks all leave their mark.

Grain whisky

Grain whisky is made mostly from unmalted cereals such as wheat or maize, with a share of malted barley to convert the starch. It is distilled in column stills, which run continuously and reach higher strengths. The result is lighter in flavour and cheaper to produce in volume.

Lighter is not the same as neutral. Scotch grain whisky must come off the still below 94.8% ABV and spend at least three years in oak, so it carries grain and cask character. A few producers bottle it on its own as single grain.

Blended whisky

Blended Scotch is a mix of malt and grain whiskies, usually from many distilleries. Grain whisky makes up the body of most blends. The malts bring the character that makes one brand taste different from another. The exact proportions are each house's own business.

There are two other blended categories. A blended malt combines malts from more than one distillery with no grain whisky; older labels called this vatted malt. A blended grain does the same with grain whiskies only.

Blends exist for a practical reason. Once column stills arrived in the nineteenth century, merchants could combine heavy malts with lighter grain whisky into something consistent and affordable. That made big national brands possible. How blenders keep a brand tasting the same is its own craft, covered in our guide to blending.

Telling them apart in the glass

Pour a blended Scotch next to a single malt and nose both at room temperature. The blend usually smells lighter and softer, with cereal, vanilla and a little fruit. The malt tends to be fuller. Its particular note stands out more clearly: pear, sherry, wax or smoke depending on the distillery and cask.

Neither result is a quality grade. A light blend is a deliberate design, built to be easy to drink with water, soda or ice. Our guide to tasting whisky properly shows how to run this comparison with a team.

How other countries use the words

Irish whiskey uses malt, grain and blended categories too. It adds single pot still, made from a mix of malted and unmalted barley in pot stills at one distillery. American whiskey sorts itself mainly by grain recipe instead: bourbon must be at least 51% corn and rye whiskey at least 51% rye.

Indian labels use the same words under Indian rules, with state rules on top. Much mainstream Indian whisky blends malt or grain spirit with neutral spirit, which can be made from molasses. Our guide to molasses and grain spirit explains the difference. The practical lesson is simple: read the category the label declares rather than assuming the Scotch meaning.

Why the split matters for cost and planning

Malt spirit generally costs more per litre of pure alcohol than grain spirit. Pot stills run in batches and malted barley costs more than unmalted wheat or maize. A change in blend recipe therefore moves cost per case. It also moves demand between different stocks, possibly years after those stocks were laid down.

A sales report shows one blended brand. Behind it sit malts of different ages from different distilleries, each with its own stock line and its own refill plan. Someone reading that report without knowing the recipe will misread a stock warning or a margin swing. That is the gap most new joiners fall into in their first year.

Common questions

Is single malt better than blended whisky?

Different, not better. A single malt shows one distillery's character. A blend is built for balance and consistency. Some of the most skilled work in the industry goes into blends.

Does single malt mean it comes from one cask?

No. Single means one distillery. Whisky from one cask is labelled single cask.

What is a blended malt?

Malt whiskies from more than one distillery mixed together, with no grain whisky. Older labels called it vatted malt.

Is grain whisky the same as vodka?

No. Scotch grain whisky is distilled below 94.8% ABV and aged at least three years in oak, so it keeps grain and wood flavour. Vodka is taken much closer to neutral.
