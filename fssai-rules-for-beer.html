FSSAI and beer

Founders often assume excise covers everything. FSSAI runs a parallel track for beer as a food product, and missing it stalls a launch just as surely as a missing excise permit.

3 min read · Updated January 2024 · Craft Beer School

Two regulators, not one

Excise departments licence the making and moving of alcohol. FSSAI, the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India, licenses and regulates it as a food product, because under Indian food law alcoholic beverages fall within FSSAI's scope for packaging, labelling and safety standards. A brewery typically needs both an excise licence from the state and an FSSAI licence or registration, and they are applied for through separate processes with separate documentation, separate fees and separate timelines.

New founders sometimes discover this only when a distributor or a large retail chain asks for the FSSAI number before listing the product, by which point it is a scramble rather than a planned step. Apply for FSSAI registration early in your setup timeline, alongside your excise application, rather than after.

What FSSAI actually checks

FSSAI's brief for a packaged beverage covers food safety management at the production site, accurate and complete labelling, and standards for any permitted additives or processing aids used in the brewing process. It also sets the licence category, basic, state or central, depending on your production volume and whether you sell across state lines, which is worth confirming early since the category determines both the fee and the application route.

A brewery that also sells food, in a brewpub for instance, needs FSSAI coverage for that side of the business too, and that requirement sits on top of, not instead of, the beverage manufacturing licence. See beer label rules in India for what FSSAI expects to see printed on the bottle or can itself.

Where this meets the brewhouse

FSSAI licensing is not purely a paperwork exercise. Inspectors can and do visit, and they look at basic food safety practice: potable water supply, pest control, staff hygiene, and traceability of ingredients and batches through the production process. A brewery that already runs disciplined batch records for its own quality control, tracking hop lot numbers, yeast generation and cleaning logs, usually sails through this part, because it is the same discipline applied to a different form.

This is one of the few compliance areas where good brewing practice and good regulatory practice are the same habit, not two competing demands on your time. Treat your quality documentation as doing double duty from the outset.

Renewals and the timeline nobody warns you about

Licences are not one-and-done. FSSAI registrations and licences run on renewal cycles, and letting one lapse can halt dispatch even if your excise paperwork is perfectly in order, since retailers and distributors will generally decline stock without a valid, current FSSAI number on file. Build renewal dates into the same calendar you use for excise permits, not a separate one you forget to check, and assign a specific person the responsibility for watching it.

A missed FSSAI renewal is a particularly frustrating way to lose sales days, because it is entirely avoidable with basic administrative discipline rather than requiring any real operational fix.

The honest bottom line

FSSAI requirements, fee categories and inspection practice vary by state and licence type, and the rules are updated from time to time, so treat any specific figure you hear secondhand as out of date until you confirm it against the current FSSAI notification or with a licensing consultant who works with breweries regularly.

Documentation that keeps both regulators satisfied at once

A shared documentation habit serves both FSSAI and excise at the same time: batch-wise production records, ingredient traceability back to supplier and lot, cleaning logs with dates and staff sign-off, and a simple incident log for anything that went wrong and how it was fixed. Neither regulator invented this discipline, quality-minded brewers were doing it before FSSAI ever asked, but it happens to be exactly what an inspector from either department wants to see.

Keep these records on paper or in a simple spreadsheet from the very first batch, not from the week before an inspection is announced, since retrofitting months of missing records convincingly is far harder than keeping them as you go.

Common questions

Do I need FSSAI as well as an excise licence?

Generally yes. Excise covers alcohol production and movement under state law, while FSSAI regulates it as a food product for safety and labelling. Most breweries need both.

Does FSSAI inspect breweries?

It can. Inspections typically look at food safety basics such as water quality, hygiene, pest control and batch traceability, similar to what good quality control already covers.

What FSSAI licence category does a brewery need?

It depends on production volume and whether you sell across state lines, ranging from basic registration to a state or central licence. Confirm your category with FSSAI or a licensing consultant.

What happens if my FSSAI registration lapses?

Distributors and retailers will typically refuse stock without a current, valid FSSAI number, so a lapsed licence can stop dispatch even if excise paperwork is otherwise in order.
