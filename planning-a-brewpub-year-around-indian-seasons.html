Planning a brewpub's year around Indian seasons

Indian demand moves with the weather and the calendar. A brewpub that brews to last week's sales will always be a step behind. Here is how we build a year plan that starts from the seasons and works back to brew day.

3 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

Why the calendar beats the recipe book

Most brewpubs plan one brew at a time. The head brewer looks at what is running low, picks the next recipe and books a brew day. That works in a steady market. India is not a steady market.

Demand swings with heat, rain, festivals, weddings, the cricket season and the dry days your state declares. A light lager that sells out in April can sit in a bright tank through a wet August. A dark beer nobody wanted in May can carry a cold Delhi December. If the tank plan only reacts to sales, the beer you need arrives three weeks after the people who wanted it.

Map your year in three seasons

We start owners with a simple split, then adjust for their city.

  • Summer. Roughly the hot months before the monsoon. Peak thirst for pale lagers, wheat beers and anything light and cold. Your chiller works hardest now too.
  • Monsoon. Footfall often dips in cities with heavy rain, especially on weeknights. Humidity threatens malt stores and power cuts test your cold side.
  • Festive and winter. From the festive run in autumn through New Year and the wedding season. Groups get bigger and spend more. In the north, darker and stronger beers find buyers.

Bengaluru's mild weather flattens these swings. Mumbai and Goa feel the monsoon hard. Delhi and Chandigarh have a real winter. Write down what your own city does, then check it against your sales history rather than memory.

Work backwards from tank time

The fixed constraint in a brewpub is fermenter space, so plan in tank weeks. A clean ale needs about two to three weeks from brew to serving. A properly lagered beer, fermented at around 10 to 12 °C and conditioned cold, needs four to six weeks and often more. That gap decides everything.

If you want a full bright tank of lager for the first warm weekend, count back six weeks from that date and protect a fermenter for it. If the festive weeks need two extra lagers and a seasonal, they must be brewing while the monsoon is still quiet. A brewpub with four fermenters has perhaps eight to ten lager slots a year once the ales are in. Spend them on purpose.

Put every planned brew on a 52-week sheet with its tank, its brew date and its date on tap. Clashes show up at once, months before they cost you an empty tap.

Raw materials and the monsoon

Malt is hygroscopic. In a humid store it picks up moisture, mills poorly and loses extract. Damp sacks also invite mould and pests. Before the rains, raise sacks off the floor on pallets, keep them away from walls and buy smaller lots more often. Hops belong in a cold store, sealed, whatever the season.

Imported malt, hops and yeast need lead time for shipping and customs clearance. Order for the festive season while it is still hot. See sourcing malt and hops in India for how we set up suppliers.

Use the slow weeks well

A quiet monsoon fortnight is the best time for the work that disrupts service: a deep clean of the cold side, gasket replacement, calibrating thermometers and flow meters, staff leave and training. Get the glycol chiller and condenser serviced before summer, not during the first heatwave when every technician in the city is booked.

Turn last year's numbers into next year's plan

The plan is only as good as the data under it. Pull weekly litres sold by beer from your POS for the last year. Mark festivals, dry days, big matches and the weeks the power failed. You will see your real seasons and they are rarely the ones the owner describes.

From there it is arithmetic: forecast litres by style for each month, convert to brews, fit brews into tank weeks. Our guide to tap list planning with sales data covers the forecasting side. Most brewpubs never get this far because nobody owns the sheet. The plan exists in the brewer's head and leaves when the brewer does.

Review the sheet monthly with the weekly numbers you already track. Move a brew early or late when the weather turns. A year plan is a working document, not a promise.

Common questions

How far ahead should a brewpub plan its brews?

At least two to three months for tank allocation and imported ingredients, with a rough 52-week view behind that. Lagers need the longest lead because they hold a fermenter for four to six weeks or more.

Do Indian seasons really change what people drink?

Yes, though the pattern differs by city. Hot months favour light, cold beers, while northern winters open room for darker and stronger styles. Your own POS history is the best guide.

How do I protect malt during the monsoon?

Store sacks on pallets away from walls in a dry, ventilated room, buy smaller lots more often and use older stock first. Damp malt mills badly and loses extract.
