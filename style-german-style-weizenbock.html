A hefeweizen with the strength and richness of a bock. Banana bread in a glass.

By Ankur Napa and Chatty Girija

Colour4.5-30 SRM
Bitterness15-35 IBU
Strength7-9.5%

Serve in a weizen glass at 8-11 °C

What it tastes like

Weizenbock is what happens when Bavarian wheat beer meets bock strength. Schneider in Munich set the template in the early 1900s with a dark, strong wheat beer, and pale versions followed later. The recipe is a hefeweizen scaled up: at least half wheat malt, the same top-fermenting weissbier yeast, and enough extra malt to reach 7 to 9 percent.

That yeast is the star. It gives banana and clove, and at this strength those notes deepen into banana bread, baked apple, plum and raisin. Dark versions add caramel and a light chocolate edge, while pale ones stay closer to honey and fresh bread. Hops barely register. The wheat gives a soft, fluffy texture and a big head, and the high carbonation keeps a rich beer from feeling heavy. Fermentation temperature decides the balance: warmer pushes banana, cooler pushes clove, and brewers tune it to taste.

Style profile

Look
Pale gold to dark mahogany, cloudy from yeast and wheat, with a thick, mousse-like head that lasts.
Aroma and flavour
Banana, clove and dark fruit such as plum and raisin over bready wheat malt. Caramel in darker versions, very little hop.
Mouthfeel
Medium-full, soft and creamy body from wheat, lively carbonation and a gentle warming finish.
Ingredients
Wheat malt as half or more of the grist, with Pilsner, Munich and some caramel malt, a little noble hop, and a weissbier yeast.

In India

Indian drinkers already know hefeweizen, so weizenbock is an easy step up. A few Bengaluru brewpubs have poured one, and it is a good beer to try fresh from the tank because the yeast character fades with age and heat. It is strong, so share a glass or take a smaller pour. It works best on a cool evening or after a long monsoon walk rather than in May sunshine.

  • Mutton pepper fryBlack pepper picks up the clove note, and the beer's malt calms the heat.
  • Banana halwaBanana meets banana, and the beer's bubbles keep the ghee in check.
