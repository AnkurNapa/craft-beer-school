Brewing degree or short course?

A degree and a short course are not competing for the same decision. They answer different questions, and knowing which question you are actually asking saves you a lot of wasted time.

3 min read · Updated September 2022 · Craft Beer School

  3. What a short course actually gives you

The two paths solve different problems

A brewing or fermentation science degree builds deep chemistry, microbiology and engineering foundations over several years. A short professional course builds working competence in a specific area, fermentation, quality control, recipe formulation, over weeks. If you are choosing between them as if they were interchangeable, you have already misunderstood what each one is for.

The better question is not which one is better, it is what you are actually trying to become. Someone aiming at a research or product development role in a large brewing company is asking a different question to someone who wants to be brewing beer on a small commercial system within six months.

What a degree actually gives you

A degree gives you depth: the biochemistry of fermentation, the engineering behind heat exchange and CIP systems, the statistics behind quality control. It also gives you time, several years of it, and in India the number of dedicated brewing degree programmes is small, so a serious academic path often means studying food technology, microbiology or chemical engineering and specialising toward brewing yourself.

That depth pays off most clearly the further you move from the brewhouse floor and toward decisions about process design, scale-up or troubleshooting a fault nobody on site has seen before. It is a foundation you draw on for a whole career, not just the first job.

What a short course actually gives you

A short course, run over weeks rather than years, gives you working knowledge fast: how to read a fermentation curve, how to calculate ABV and attenuation, how to run a basic sensory panel, how a small brewhouse actually operates day to day. It will not make you a food scientist, but it can get someone who is already working, or already homebrewing seriously, to a level where a brewery will consider them for an entry role.

What breweries in India are actually hiring for

Most Indian craft breweries are small teams where the assistant brewer or junior brewer role is learned largely on the floor, under a head brewer, regardless of what qualification got someone in the door. A degree signals depth that matters more as you move toward a head brewer or quality lead role later; a short course plus real hands-on experience, including your own homebrewing, is often enough to get the first job.

Time and cost are the honest comparison

A degree is a multi-year commitment, in time and cost, and makes most sense if you are early in your career or want to eventually move into brewing science, product development or a larger industrial brewery. A short course is a matter of weeks and gets you moving faster, which matters if you are changing careers and need to test the fit before committing years to it.

A sensible sequence, not a single choice

Many working brewers we know started with a short course or serious homebrewing, took an entry-level role, and only later decided whether a formal science qualification was worth the additional years. Starting on the floor, then deciding, is a lower-risk path than committing to a multi-year degree before you know whether the day-to-day work suits you.

The floor also tells you something a syllabus cannot: whether you actually enjoy the physical, repetitive, early-morning reality of production brewing, as distinct from enjoying the idea of it. That distinction only becomes obvious once you have lived a few brew days.

What actually gets you hired either way

Whichever path you take, a brewery hiring an entry-level brewer wants to see that you understand the process, can follow procedure precisely, and can taste and describe beer accurately. A course, short or long, is the fastest way to prove that on paper. What happens in the interview and on a trial shift is what actually seals it.

Bring specifics to that conversation: what you brewed, what went wrong and how you fixed it, what a course taught you that you have since applied. A candidate who can talk concretely about a stuck fermentation or a hazy IPA that would not clear stands out far more than one who lists a certificate without being able to discuss what is behind it. That kind of specific, honest detail is what a trial shift is designed to confirm anyway.

Common questions

Can I become a brewer without any formal qualification?

Yes, some brewers learn entirely on the job, but a short course or documented homebrewing experience makes it easier to get that first opportunity, since it shows a brewery you already understand the basics.

Is a brewing degree available in India?

Dedicated brewing degrees are rare in India. Many brewers instead study food technology, microbiology or chemical engineering and specialise toward brewing through short courses and hands-on work.

Does a short course guarantee a job?

No course guarantees a job. It builds the working knowledge that makes you a stronger candidate, but the role still depends on the brewery's needs and your performance on a trial or interview.

Should I do the degree first or try a short course first?

If you are not sure brewing is the career for you, a short course is the lower-risk way to test the fit before committing several years to a degree.
