Bottling straight from the fermenter with a bottling wand

Many homebrewers bottle straight from the fermenter, skipping the bottling bucket entirely. It saves a transfer and some washing up. Here is how to do it with a bottling wand and get even carbonation in every bottle.

3 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

Why bottle from the fermenter

The classic method racks beer into a bottling bucket, stirs in priming sugar then fills from the bucket's tap. It works. It also means one more vessel to clean and sanitise and one more transfer where the beer can splash and pick up oxygen. Bottling straight from the fermenter removes that step.

You need a fermenter with a tap set slightly above the bottom, a short length of tubing and a bottling wand. Most plastic homebrew fermenters sold in India come with a tap already fitted. If yours is a carboy with no tap, an auto-siphon with the wand on the end does the same job.

Check where the tap sits before you rely on it. A tap that sits 2 or 3 cm above the base clears the yeast bed in most batches. A tap set flush with the bottom will draw sediment from the first bottle.

How a bottling wand works

A bottling wand is a rigid plastic tube with a spring-loaded or weighted valve at the tip. Press the tip on the bottom of the bottle and the valve opens. Lift it and the flow stops. Beer fills from the bottom up, so it does not splash through the air.

The best part is the headspace. Fill until beer reaches the very top of the bottle, then lift the wand out. The space the wand took up becomes the headspace, usually about 2 to 3 cm in a standard bottle. That gives every bottle the same gap, which means even carbonation and fewer surprises.

Priming has to happen bottle by bottle

The catch is priming sugar. In a bottling bucket you stir a sugar solution evenly through the whole batch. Stir sugar into a fermenter and you disturb the yeast bed you are trying to leave behind. So when you bottle from the fermenter, you prime each bottle individually.

  • Carbonation drops. Pre-measured sugar tablets, usually one per 330 ml bottle and two per 650 ml bottle. Simple and consistent.
  • A measured spoon of sugar. Cheap but slow. It is also easy to get wrong across 60 bottles.
  • A dosed sugar syrup. A syringe or dosing bottle delivers the same volume of sugar solution into each bottle before filling.

Our comparison of carbonation drops versus batch priming goes into the trade-offs.

Keep the yeast where it is

Move the fermenter to its bottling spot a day before you bottle. Moving it on the day stirs up the yeast and trub. Cold crashing for a couple of days first compacts the sediment further. Tilt the fermenter very slightly forward with a book under the back edge if you want the last litre, but expect some cloudiness near the end.

Run the first 100 ml or so into a glass and discard it. It flushes any sediment that settled in the tap. Then attach the tube and wand and start filling. If the beer turns cloudy late in the batch, stop. A litre left behind is better than 10 bottles with a thick yeast layer.

A routine for bottling day

  1. Confirm the gravity has held steady for three days. Bottling before fermentation ends is the main cause of gushers.
  2. Clean and sanitise every bottle, the tap, tubing and wand. Our bottling day checklist covers the full list.
  3. Add the priming sugar to each bottle.
  4. Fill each bottle to the top with the wand, lift out and cap straight away.
  5. Invert each bottle gently once or twice to dissolve the sugar.
  6. Store at 20 to 24 °C for two to three weeks.

A 20 litre batch fills about 30 bottles of 650 ml or 60 bottles of 330 ml. With a helper capping as you fill, an hour is realistic.

Small details decide the batch

Bottling looks like the boring end of brewing. It is where carbonation, oxygen and contamination are all decided in one afternoon. If you are still choosing a fermenter, our guide comparing buckets, carboys and stainless fermenters covers which ones make tap bottling easy.

Your first step: before your next bottling day, buy a bottling wand to fit your fermenter tap and a pack of carbonation drops. Fill three bottles from the tap with water to practise the headspace before the real beer goes in.

Common questions

Do I need a bottling bucket?

No. If your fermenter has a tap above the yeast bed, a bottling wand lets you fill directly. You then prime each bottle individually.

How much headspace should I leave?

Fill to the top with a bottling wand and lift it out. The space the wand displaced, about 2 to 3 cm, is the right headspace.

Will bottling from the fermenter make cloudy beer?

Only if you disturb the sediment. Move the fermenter a day early, cold crash if you can and stop filling if the beer turns cloudy.
