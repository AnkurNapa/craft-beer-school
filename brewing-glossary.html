Brewing glossary.

295 brewing, tasting, packaging, business and wine terms, defined in plain English for brewers and drinkers in India. Search, filter by topic or jump to a letter.

ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUVWYZ

Showing all 295 terms

A

ABV Fermentation and conditioning
Share of a beer's volume that is ethanol, shown as a percentage. Brewers estimate it from gravity readings with ABV = (OG - FG) x 131.25, using specific gravity. Read the guideAlso: Alcohol by volume
Acetaldehyde Fermentation and conditioning
Green apple compound yeast makes on the way to alcohol and normally reabsorbs. Noticeable levels point to beer pulled off yeast too early, under-pitching or oxidation. Read the guide
Acidulated malt Ingredients
Malt carrying lactic acid from lactic bacteria grown on it during malting. A few per cent of the grist lowers mash pH into range without adding mineral acid. Read the guideAlso: Sauermalz
Adjunct Ingredients
Any unmalted source of fermentable sugar, such as rice, maize or sugar, used alongside malt. Adjuncts lighten body and flavour, lower cost and are common in Indian mainstream lagers. Read the guide
Age statement Wine and spirits
Number on a whisky label giving the age of the youngest spirit in the bottle. A 12-year-old can contain older whisky but nothing younger. Read the guide
Ale Tasting and styles
Beer fermented with top-fermenting ale yeast at warmer temperatures, often 15 to 22°C (59 to 72°F). Ales tend to show more fruity esters than lagers. Read the guide
Ale yeast Ingredients
Strains of Saccharomyces cerevisiae that ferment warm, typically 15 to 24 °C (59 to 75 °F), producing fruity esters. Many rise to the top during fermentation, hence the old name top-fermenting yeast. Read the guideAlso: Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Top-fermenting yeast
Alkalinity Ingredients
Ability of water to resist a drop in pH, mainly from bicarbonate, expressed as mg/L of calcium carbonate. High alkalinity pushes mash pH up, which suits dark beers more than pale ones. Read the guideAlso: Total alkalinity
Alpha acids Ingredients
Resins in hop lupulin that become bitter once isomerised in the boil. The alpha acid percentage on a hop packet is how brewers calculate the dose for a target bitterness. Read the guideAlso: Humulones
Alpha amylase Brewhouse
Mash enzyme that chops starch chains at random points, most active around 68 to 72 °C (154 to 162 °F). It liquefies starch quickly, leaving dextrins as well as fermentable sugars. Read the guide
Angels' share Wine and spirits
Spirit lost to evaporation through the cask during maturation. Losses are far higher in hot climates such as India than in Scotland, which speeds ageing but costs volume. Read the guide
Aroma Tasting and styles
What a beer smells like, sensed through the nose before and while drinking. Most of what people call taste is really aroma, so judges assess it first. Read the guide
Astringency Tasting and styles
Dry, puckering, tea-like roughness on the palate caused by tannins and other polyphenols. A little suits some roasty beers; too much usually points to harsh husk extraction. Read the guide
Attenuation Fermentation and conditioning
Drop in gravity during fermentation, shown as a percentage of the original gravity. Most brewing strains land roughly between 65 and 85 per cent, which shapes body and dryness. Read the guideAlso: Apparent attenuation

B

Barrel-aged Tasting and styles
Beer matured in wooden barrels, often ex-bourbon, wine or rum, picking up vanilla, oak, spirit or wine notes. Ageing takes months and the wood can host souring microbes. Read the guide
Base malt Ingredients
Pale, highly enzymatic malt such as pilsner or pale ale malt that makes up most of a grist. It provides the bulk of the fermentable extract plus the enzymes to convert it. Read the guide
Batch code Packaging and quality
Printed code on a can, bottle or keg that ties it to a brew, tank and packaging run. Without it a recall or complaint investigation becomes guesswork. Read the guideAlso: lot code
Beer loss Business and India
Volume lost between brewhouse and sale through tank bottoms, filling waste, breakages and dumped batches. Tracking it stage by stage is one of the cheapest profit gains. Read the guideAlso: yield loss
Berliner Weisse Tasting and styles
Low-strength, very pale, sharply tart wheat beer from Berlin, often around 3% ABV. It was traditionally served with flavoured syrups to soften the acidity. Read the guide
Best before date Packaging and quality
Date on a label up to which the producer expects the drink to keep its quality when stored as directed. It is a quality promise, not a safety cut-off. Read the guide
Beta acids Ingredients
Hop resins that barely isomerise in the boil, so they add little bitterness at first. They can oxidise over time into bitter compounds and they have antibacterial activity. Read the guideAlso: Lupulones
Beta amylase Brewhouse
Mash enzyme that snips maltose from the ends of starch chains, most active around 60 to 65 °C (140 to 149 °F). It is heat sensitive, so cooler mashes give drier, more fermentable worts. Read the guide
Beta-glucan rest Brewhouse
Rest around 35 to 45 °C (95 to 113 °F) where beta-glucanase breaks down gums from grain cell walls. It eases lautering in grists high in oats, rye or unmalted barley. Read the guide
Beta-glucanase Brewhouse
Enzyme that breaks down beta-glucans, the sticky gums in grain cell walls. Without enough of it wort turns viscous, lautering slows and beer can haze or filter poorly. Read the guide
Biotransformation Ingredients
Chemical changes yeast makes to hop compounds during fermentation, such as converting geraniol or freeing bound thiols. It is why dry hopping during active fermentation tastes different from dry hopping afterwards. Read the guide
Bitterness units Packaging and quality
Lab measure of bitterness made by extracting acidified beer with iso-octane and reading absorbance at 275 nm. It tracks bitter hop compounds, not how bitter a beer actually tastes. Read the guideAlso: BU, IBU measurement
BJCP Tasting and styles
US non-profit that writes widely used beer style guidelines and certifies beer judges through tasting exams. Its guidelines are the reference at most homebrew competitions. Read the guideAlso: Beer Judge Certification Program
Blended whisky Wine and spirits
Whisky made by mixing different whiskies, typically malt and grain. Many Indian blends also include neutral spirit, which is why labels and definitions differ by market. Read the guide
Bock Tasting and styles
Strong German lager, roughly 6 to 7.5% ABV, with rich toasty malt and little hop character. Doppelbock, maibock and eisbock are stronger or seasonal relatives. Read the guide
Body Tasting and styles
Perceived weight or fullness of a beer on the palate, from thin and watery to thick and chewy. Residual sugars, proteins and alcohol all add to it. Read the guide
Boil Brewhouse
Vigorous boiling of the wort, usually for 60 to 90 minutes. It sterilises the wort, isomerises hop acids, drives off DMS and coagulates protein. Read the guideAlso: Wort boiling
Bonded warehouse Business and India
Store where alcohol is held under excise supervision before duty is paid. Duty usually falls due as stock leaves bond, though procedures vary by state. Read the guide
Bottle Packaging and quality
Glass container for beer, most often brown because amber glass blocks most of the light that causes skunky flavour. Green and clear glass give little protection. Read the guide
Bottle conditioning Fermentation and conditioning
Carbonating beer in the bottle with a small dose of priming sugar plus live yeast. It leaves a fine yeast sediment and the yeast helps mop up oxygen. Read the guide
Brewhouse capacity Business and India
Volume of wort a brewhouse can produce per brew, usually given in hectolitres. Annual output also depends on brews per day and how much fermenting space follows. Read the guide
Brewhouse efficiency Brewhouse
Share of the malt's potential extract that reaches the fermenter as wort. It is how breweries judge milling, mashing and lautering, since losses show up as wasted malt. Read the guide
Brewing sugar Ingredients
Simple sugars such as sucrose, dextrose or invert sugar added in the kettle to raise alcohol without adding body. Belgian brewers use them to keep strong beers dry and drinkable. Read the guideAlso: Dextrose, Candi sugar
Brewpub Business and India
Restaurant or bar that brews beer on site and serves it there, usually straight from serving tanks. In several Indian states it is the main craft beer model. Read the guide
Bright beer tank Fermentation and conditioning
Pressure-rated tank that holds finished, clarified beer ready for packaging or serving. In brewpubs beer is often poured straight from it.Also: BBT, Serving tank
Brix Brewhouse
Sugar concentration scale read on refractometers, close to Plato for unfermented wort. Once alcohol is present, refractometer readings need a correction formula.Also: Degrees Brix
Bulk litre Business and India
Volume of a drink as measured, whatever its strength. Indian excise often counts beer in bulk litres and spirits in proof litres; methods vary by state. Read the guideAlso: BL

C

Calcium Ingredients
Most important brewing mineral, lowering mash pH, protecting enzymes and helping yeast settle. Many brewers aim for at least 50 mg/L in their brewing water. Read the guide
Can Packaging and quality
Aluminium container with a thin inner lining, sealed by a lid seamed onto the body. Cans block all light and weigh little, though filling must be tightly controlled to keep oxygen out. Read the guide
Carbonation Fermentation and conditioning
Carbon dioxide dissolved in beer, giving fizz, lift to aroma and a livelier mouthfeel. Its level is set by temperature and pressure, whether produced naturally or forced in. Read the guide
Carbonic bite Tasting and styles
Prickly, slightly acidic tingle on the tongue as dissolved CO2 forms carbonic acid. Higher carbonation makes beer feel lighter and sharper; low carbonation feels softer and fuller. Read the guideAlso: carbonation perception
Case Business and India
Standard selling unit of packaged drinks, such as 12 bottles of 650 ml or 24 cans of 500 ml. Case sizes differ, so convert to litres before comparing sales. Read the guide
Cask Wine and spirits
Wooden container, usually oak, used to mature spirits and wine. Ex-bourbon and ex-sherry casks are the most common for whisky, each shaping colour and flavour. Read the guideAlso: barrel
Cask ale Packaging and quality
Unfiltered ale that finishes conditioning in its cask and is served without added gas pressure, usually by hand pump. Once tapped it must be sold within days because air replaces the beer drawn off.Also: real ale
Cask strength Wine and spirits
Whisky bottled at the strength it comes out of the cask, without dilution with water, often above 50% ABV. Read the guideAlso: barrel proof
Centrifuge Fermentation and conditioning
Machine spinning beer at high speed so yeast and solids are thrown out by centrifugal force. It clarifies quickly with little beer loss, useful for hazy beers that need partial clarity. Read the guide
Cereal cooker Brewhouse
Separate vessel that boils rice or maize with a little malt to gelatinise the starch before it joins the main mash. It is standard kit in breweries that brew with rice or maize grits. Read the guideAlso: Adjunct cooker
Chill haze Fermentation and conditioning
Cloudiness that appears when beer is cold and clears as it warms, caused by protein and polyphenol complexes. Over time it can turn into permanent haze. Read the guide
Cicerone Tasting and styles
Certification programme for beer service and knowledge, running from Certified Beer Server up to Master Cicerone. It focuses on serving, storing, styles and food pairing. Read the guide
CIP Brewhouse
Cleaning tanks and pipes by pumping hot caustic, acid and rinse water through fixed spray balls and lines without taking them apart. It is the backbone of brewery hygiene. Read the guideAlso: Cleaning in place
Cleaning Packaging and quality
Removing soil, protein, beer stone and other deposits from a surface, usually with caustic or acid detergents. A surface must be clean before it can be sanitised. Read the guide
Cleaning in place Packaging and quality
Cleaning tanks and pipework by pumping detergent and rinse cycles through them without taking them apart. Time, temperature, concentration and flow together decide whether it works. Read the guideAlso: CIP
COGS Business and India
Direct cost of making the product sold: ingredients, packaging, direct labour and production overheads. Companies treat excise differently within it, so check before comparing figures. Read the guideAlso: cost of goods sold
Cohumulone Ingredients
One of the main alpha acid analogues, usually quoted as a percentage of total alpha acids. Many brewers believe high-cohumulone hops give a harsher bitterness, though the evidence is debated. Read the guide
Cold break Brewhouse
Fine protein and polyphenol particles that drop out of wort when it is chilled quickly. Fast cooling improves it, giving clearer beer. Read the guide
Cold crash Fermentation and conditioning
Rapidly chilling finished beer to near 0 °C (32 °F) to drop yeast, protein and hop particles out of suspension. It speeds clarity before packaging or filtration. Read the guideAlso: Crash cooling
Cold IPA Tasting and styles
Crisp, dry IPA fermented with lager yeast at warmer temperatures, often with rice or maize in the grist. It pairs West Coast hop character with lager cleanness. Read the guide
Column still Wine and spirits
Tall still that distils continuously over many internal plates, producing a lighter, stronger spirit efficiently. It is used for grain whisky, vodka and most Indian molasses spirit. Read the guideAlso: continuous still, Coffey still
Conditioning Fermentation and conditioning
Period after primary fermentation when beer matures, clears and carbonates. Yeast cleans up off-flavours such as diacetyl and acetaldehyde during this time. Read the guideAlso: Maturation, Secondary
Contract brewing Business and India
Arrangement where a brand owner pays an established brewery to brew and package its recipe. It lets brands launch without building a plant, though quality depends on the host. Read the guide
Control chart Packaging and quality
Time-ordered plot of a measurement with a centre line and statistical control limits. Points outside the limits or unusual runs show a process has shifted and needs attention. Read the guide
Cork taint Wine and spirits
Musty, damp cardboard smell caused by trichloroanisole, often from a contaminated cork. Even tiny amounts mute a wine's fruit. Read the guideAlso: TCA
Cost per litre Business and India
Total cost of making one litre of finished beer, including ingredients, packaging, energy, labour and overheads. Pricing and margin decisions should start from this number. Read the guide
Craft beer Tasting and styles
Beer from smaller, independent breweries that prize flavour, variety and local character. India has no single legal definition, so the term is used loosely. Read the guide
Crown Packaging and quality
Crimped metal cap that seals a beer bottle, with a liner that grips the glass. Some liners absorb oxygen, which helps slow staling in the bottle.Also: crown cork, crown cap
Cryo hops Ingredients
Lupulin separated from cone material at very low temperature, then pelletised, giving roughly twice the resin content of standard pellets. Less leafy matter means less beer lost to hop debris. Read the guideAlso: Lupulin powder, Lupulin pellets
Crystal malt Ingredients
Speciality malt stewed while wet so its starch turns to sugar inside the husk before kilning or roasting. It adds sweetness, body plus caramel to toffee flavours, with colour set by how hard it is roasted. Read the guideAlso: Caramel malt
Cut points Wine and spirits
Moments when a distiller switches the spirit run from heads to hearts and from hearts to tails. Only the hearts are kept, so cut timing shapes flavour. Read the guideAlso: heads, hearts and tails
Cylindroconical fermenter Fermentation and conditioning
Tall fermenter with a cone at the base where yeast and trub collect. It is standard in modern breweries because yeast is easy to remove and harvest.Also: CCT, Fermentation vessel, FV

D

Decoction mash Brewhouse
Mash method where part of the mash is drawn off, boiled then returned to raise the temperature. It deepens malt flavour and colour, the traditional route for many German and Czech lagers. Read the guideAlso: Decoction
Degrees Plato Packaging and quality
Brewing scale giving the percentage by weight of dissolved extract in wort or beer, so 12°P means 12 g of extract per 100 g of liquid. Most commercial breweries record gravity this way. Read the guideAlso: °P, Plato
Depletions Business and India
Volume a distributor sells on to retailers and bars, a closer signal of real demand than brewery shipments. Comparing the two shows stock building up or running down in the trade. Read the guide
Dextrins Brewhouse
Longer chains of glucose that most brewing yeast cannot ferment. They stay in the beer as body with a little sweetness; higher mash temperatures leave more of them. Read the guide
Diacetyl Fermentation and conditioning
Buttery, butterscotch compound formed from a yeast by-product during fermentation. Healthy yeast reabsorbs it if given time, so high levels usually mean rushed fermentation or infection. Read the guide
Diacetyl rest Fermentation and conditioning
Warming beer a few degrees near the end of fermentation, often to around 16 to 18 °C (61 to 64 °F) for lagers, so yeast clears diacetyl faster. It is standard before lagering. Read the guide
Diastatic power Ingredients
Measure of the starch-converting enzyme activity in a malt, given in degrees Lintner or WK. It tells you whether a grist has enough enzymes to convert adjuncts and speciality malts. Read the guideAlso: DP
Diastaticus Ingredients
STA1-positive variant of brewing yeast that slowly ferments dextrins and starch. In packaged beer it causes over-carbonation, gushing and burst bottles, so breweries treat it as a serious contaminant. Read the guideAlso: Saccharomyces cerevisiae var. diastaticus
Diatomaceous earth Fermentation and conditioning
Powdered fossil algae used as a filter aid, building a porous cake that traps yeast and haze. It works well but needs careful handling because the dust harms lungs. Read the guideAlso: DE, Kieselguhr
Dissolved oxygen Fermentation and conditioning
Oxygen dissolved in finished beer, usually measured in parts per billion. Keeping it low after fermentation is the biggest single guard against stale flavours. Read the guideAlso: DO
DMS Brewhouse
Sulphur compound tasting of cooked sweetcorn, formed from SMM in pale malt when wort is heated. A vigorous, uncovered boil drives it off; slow chilling lets more form. Read the guideAlso: Dimethyl sulphide
Double IPA Tasting and styles
Stronger, more intensely hopped IPA, typically around 7.5 to 10% ABV. It needs extra malt to carry the hops without tasting harsh. Read the guideAlso: DIPA, imperial IPA
Draught line cleaning Packaging and quality
Regular flushing of beer lines, couplers and taps with line cleaner to remove yeast, bacteria and scale. Dirty lines are a common reason good beer tastes bad at the bar. Read the guideAlso: line cleaning
Dry day Business and India
Day when the sale of alcohol is banned, such as certain national holidays, election days and some festivals. State governments set the dates, so taprooms plan stock and staff around them. Read the guide
Dry hopping Fermentation and conditioning
Adding hops to beer during or after fermentation for aroma without much extra bitterness. Timing, dose and contact time all shape the result. Read the guide
Dry state Business and India
State where the sale and consumption of alcohol is prohibited, wholly or largely. Prohibition policies change over time, so check the current position state by state. Read the guide

E

EBC colour Packaging and quality
European Brewery Convention scale for beer and malt colour, read from light absorbance at 430 nm. EBC values are roughly double SRM values, which helps when comparing recipes. Read the guideAlso: EBC
Esters Fermentation and conditioning
Fruity compounds such as isoamyl acetate (banana) and ethyl acetate (pear, solvent) made by yeast. Warmer, higher-gravity fermentations and low pitching rates raise them. Read the guide
Excise duty Business and India
Tax charged by a state on alcohol made or sold within it, collected through permits, labels or volume-based charges. Structures and processes vary by state, so brewers need local advice. Read the guide
Extra neutral alcohol Wine and spirits
Highly rectified spirit of about 95 to 96% ABV with little flavour of its own. In India it is the base of most mass-market spirits, usually made from molasses or grain. Read the guideAlso: ENA, neutral spirit
Extract Brewhouse
Dissolved solids, mostly sugars, in wort or released from malt. Malt specifications list extract as the percentage of dry weight a standard lab mash recovers. Read the guide

F

FAN Ingredients
Amino acids and small peptides in wort that yeast uses to build new cells. An all-malt wort usually has plenty; heavy adjunct use dilutes it and can stress the yeast. Read the guideAlso: Free amino nitrogen
Feni Wine and spirits
Goan spirit distilled from fermented cashew apple juice or coconut palm toddy, traditionally in small pot stills. Cashew feni holds a geographical indication tag.
Fermentation Fermentation and conditioning
Yeast turning wort sugars into alcohol, carbon dioxide and flavour compounds. Controlling its temperature does more for beer quality than almost anything else a brewer does. Read the guide
Fill height Packaging and quality
Level to which a bottle or can is filled, checked against the declared volume. Short fills shortchange the drinker and overfills waste beer, so lines check it every shift.Also: fill level
Filtration Fermentation and conditioning
Passing beer through a medium such as diatomaceous earth, sheets or membranes to remove yeast and haze. It gives bright, stable beer with a longer shelf life. Read the guide
Final gravity Fermentation and conditioning
Gravity once fermentation is complete. Together with original gravity it gives alcohol content through ABV = (OG - FG) x 131.25. Read the guideAlso: FG, Terminal gravity
Fining (wine) Wine and spirits
Adding an agent such as bentonite, egg white or gelatin to wine so it binds haze-forming or harsh compounds that then settle out. Some agents make a wine unsuitable for vegans. Read the guide
Finings Fermentation and conditioning
Clarifying agents such as isinglass, gelatine or silica sol that bind to yeast or haze particles and drag them down. They speed up clarity without filtration. Read the guide
Finish Tasting and styles
Flavours and sensations left after swallowing, described as short or long, dry or sweet, bitter or clean. A good finish invites the next sip. Read the guideAlso: aftertaste
First runnings Brewhouse
Strongest, most concentrated wort drained from the mash before sparging begins. Its gravity tells you how well the mash converted. Read the guide
Flagship beer Business and India
Core beer a brewery is best known for and sells all year round. Its steady volume usually pays for the seasonal experiments. Read the guide
Flavour wheel Tasting and styles
Chart that groups beer flavour terms from broad classes at the centre to specific descriptors at the rim. It gives tasters and brewers a shared vocabulary. Read the guide
Flocculation Ingredients
Tendency of yeast cells to clump together then drop out of suspension once fermentation slows. Highly flocculent strains clear quickly but may stop early, while low flocculators stay in suspension longer. Read the guide
Flor Wine and spirits
Film of yeast that grows on fino and manzanilla sherry in barrels left part full. It shields the wine from oxygen and gives a tangy, nutty, bread-dough character. Read the guide
Fobbing Packaging and quality
Tiny jet of hot water or CO2 squirted into a bottle just before capping so the beer foams up and drives air out of the headspace. It is a cheap way to cut oxygen pickup. Read the guideAlso: jetting
Forced ageing Packaging and quality
Test that stores sealed beer warm, commonly at 30°C (86°F) or higher, for days or weeks to predict how it will taste months later on the shelf. Read the guideAlso: accelerated ageing
Forced carbonation Fermentation and conditioning
Adding CO2 under pressure to cold beer in a tank or keg. It is faster and more controllable than natural carbonation. Read the guideAlso: Force carbonation
Fortification Wine and spirits
Adding grape spirit to wine during or after fermentation, raising alcohol to roughly 15 to 22% ABV. Port, sherry and Madeira are made this way. Read the guideAlso: fortified wine
FSSAI Business and India
India's national food regulator, which sets standards for alcoholic drinks covering composition, additives and labelling. Breweries need its licence alongside state excise approvals. Read the guideAlso: Food Safety and Standards Authority of India
Fusel alcohols Fermentation and conditioning
Heavier alcohols made by yeast that give hot, solvent-like warmth. Fermenting too warm or under-pitching increases them. Read the guideAlso: Higher alcohols, Fusels

G

Gelatinisation Brewhouse
Swelling and bursting of starch granules in hot water, which opens the starch to enzyme attack. Barley malt starch gelatinises at normal mash temperatures, whereas rice and maize need more heat. Read the guide
Gin botanicals Wine and spirits
Plant ingredients that flavour gin, led by juniper, which must dominate, plus coriander, citrus peel, angelica and many others. Indian gins often add local spices. Read the guideAlso: botanicals
Glycol Fermentation and conditioning
Food-grade coolant chilled and pumped through tank jackets to hold fermentation and storage temperatures. A properly sized glycol system is essential in hot Indian conditions. Read the guideAlso: Glycol chiller
Gose Tasting and styles
Tart German wheat beer brewed with salt and coriander, first from Goslar and later Leipzig. Light and refreshing, it suits hot weather. Read the guide
Grist Brewhouse
Mixture of milled malt and other grains ready for mashing. The grist bill lists each grain with its share, usually given in recipes as percentages. Read the guideAlso: Grain bill, Grist bill
Gross margin Business and India
Revenue minus cost of goods sold, often shown as a percentage of revenue. It shows how much each sale contributes towards overheads and profit. Read the guide
Growth phase Fermentation and conditioning
Stage after the lag phase when yeast multiplies rapidly and ferments most of the sugar. Much of the beer's ester and fusel character forms here, so temperature control matters most. Read the guideAlso: Exponential phase, Log phase
Gueuze Tasting and styles
Blend of young and old lambic, bottled so the young beer referments and builds a lively sparkle. It is dry, sour and complex. Read the guide
Gypsum Ingredients
Brewing salt that adds calcium plus sulphate, lowering mash pH and giving a crisper, drier hop finish. It is the classic addition for Burton-style pale ales. Read the guideAlso: Calcium sulphate
Gypsy brewing Business and India
Brewing by a brand or brewer with no plant of its own, renting spare capacity at other breweries batch by batch. It is a common way to test demand before investing. Read the guideAlso: cuckoo brewing, nomad brewing

H

Hardness Ingredients
Total calcium and magnesium in water, usually given as mg/L of calcium carbonate. Calcium in particular helps enzymes, yeast flocculation and beer clarity. Read the guideAlso: Water hardness
Haze Packaging and quality
Cloudiness in beer from protein, polyphenols, yeast, starch or bacteria. Some styles want it; in a beer meant to be bright it is a stability fault. Read the guide
Head retention Tasting and styles
How long the foam on a poured beer lasts before collapsing. Grease, detergent residue on glasses or poor foam proteins can kill it quickly. Read the guide
Headspace Packaging and quality
Gas space between the beer and the closure in a bottle or can. Air left there is a major source of package oxygen, so fillers work hard to push it out. Read the guide
Heat exchanger Brewhouse
Stack of metal plates that cools hot wort against cold water or glycol flowing the other way. It brings wort to pitching temperature in one pass while recovering hot water. Read the guideAlso: Plate chiller, Wort cooler
Hectolitre Business and India
Unit of 100 litres, the standard way breweries express batch size, tank volume and annual output. A 10 hl brewhouse makes about 1,000 litres per brew. Read the guideAlso: hl
Hefeweizen Tasting and styles
Unfiltered Bavarian wheat beer with banana and clove aromas from its special yeast. It is usually at least half wheat malt and served cloudy in a tall vase-shaped glass. Read the guideAlso: Weissbier
Helles Tasting and styles
Pale Munich lager that leans towards soft, bready malt rather than hop bitterness. Clean and gentle, it was created in 1894 as Bavaria's answer to pilsner. Read the guide
High krausen Fermentation and conditioning
Peak of fermentation, when the krausen is tallest and activity most vigorous. Brewers often time dry hopping, krausening and temperature changes around it. Read the guide
Hop burn Fermentation and conditioning
Harsh, scratchy, peppery feeling from hop particles left in suspension in heavily dry-hopped beers. Cold crashing and time help them settle. Read the guide
Hop creep Fermentation and conditioning
Renewed fermentation set off when enzymes in dry hops break dextrins into fermentable sugars. It can raise ABV, diacetyl and pressure in tanks or packages. Read the guide
Hop extract Ingredients

Single-celled fungus that ferments wort sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide while producing many flavour compounds. Brewing strains are mostly Saccharomyces species, chosen for flavour and reliability. Read the guide
Yeast harvesting Fermentation and conditioning
Collecting healthy yeast from a fermenter cone to pitch into a later batch. Breweries track generations and viability so they know when to start fresh. Read the guideAlso: Yeast cropping, Repitching
Yield Brewhouse
Amount of extract or wort obtained from a given weight of malt. Brewers track it every brew because a small drop adds up to large malt costs over a year. Read the guideAlso: Brewhouse yield

Z

Zinc Ingredients
Trace mineral yeast needs for healthy enzyme activity and growth. Wort is often short of it, so brewers commonly add a small dose of around 0.1 to 0.2 mg/L to the kettle. Read the guide
