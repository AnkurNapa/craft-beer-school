Seasonal and limited releases

Seasonal and limited beers can build genuine anticipation, but only if scarcity and timing are real. Manufactured scarcity is easy to spot and easy to distrust.

3 min read · Updated April 2024 · Craft Beer School

On this page

  1. Scarcity has to be genuine
  2. Tie seasonal beers to something Indian drinkers actually notice
  3. Do not let seasonal releases cannibalise the core range
  4. Give the release a defined start and end
  5. Use the release calendar to plan packaging and labour ahead of time
  6. Make the release an event, not just a shelf appearance

Scarcity has to be genuine

Calling a beer limited when you can brew another batch whenever demand justifies it is a marketing claim rather than a fact, and regular drinkers notice the difference over time. A limited release should be limited by something real: a small batch size, an ingredient available for only part of the year, or a recipe you genuinely do not plan to repeat because a tank is needed for the core range again the following week and there is simply no capacity to repeat the batch even if demand justified it.

Once a customer catches a brewery calling something limited that reappears three months later under the same name, every future limited release gets read with suspicion, and that scepticism is hard to undo once it sets in among your most attentive customers.

Decide the real constraint before you write the label copy. If you cannot name one, the beer is not limited, it is simply new, and it should be marketed as that instead, which is still a perfectly good reason to give it attention.

Tie seasonal beers to something Indian drinkers actually notice

A winter warmer built around jaggery or a monsoon session ale built to be lower in alcohol and easier to drink through a humid evening ties the beer to a season people already feel, rather than importing a seasonal calendar built for a different climate that was designed around a different set of harvests and holidays.

Mango, a fruit with a genuinely short and celebrated Indian season, is an obvious and honest seasonal ingredient if the timing and sourcing are handled properly, and the same logic applies to jackfruit, guava or any regional fruit with a real, short window rather than one available year round through cold storage. The shorter and more genuine the window, the more the release earns the word seasonal rather than borrowing it as decoration.

Do not let seasonal releases cannibalise the core range

If your seasonal beer consistently outsells your flagship, that is useful information about what your market actually wants, not a reason to panic, but it does mean the core range may need reconsidering rather than being left untouched out of habit. Watch sell-through of your regular beers during a seasonal release window rather than assuming the two are unrelated, because a strong seasonal launch can quietly starve your everyday range of taproom attention and shelf space at the same time, leaving your flagship weaker once the seasonal beer moves on.

Give the release a defined start and end

An announced window, even a rough one like through the monsoon or while stock lasts, gives a limited release its shape. Without a clear end point, a seasonal beer just becomes an occasional beer, and occasional beers rarely build the anticipation that a genuinely time-bound one does, because there is never a moment where a customer feels they might actually miss it. A hard end date, even an approximate one, creates that small, useful pressure.

Communicate the end date on the label or the tap list itself, not only in a social post that most buyers will never see before the beer is gone.

Use the release calendar to plan packaging and labour ahead of time

Seasonal releases often need their own label artwork and sometimes a different can format, and both take lead time. Our packaging costs guide covers what drives that timeline, and planning your seasonal calendar a year ahead avoids the scramble of designing a label the week before the ingredient is ready to brew with, when there is no room left to fix a mistake or reorder a label that came back wrong.

Make the release an event, not just a shelf appearance

A seasonal release paired with a taproom event, as covered in our taproom events guide, gets talked about far more than one that simply appears on the shelf with no announcement at all. Regulars who feel like they got to it first are your most reliable source of word of mouth for the next one, far more effective than any paid promotion could be given the restrictions on alcohol advertising in India, and it costs the brewery nothing beyond the pours already planned for the event.

Common questions

How many seasonal releases should a small brewery run per year?

Two to four, tied to genuine seasonal ingredients or occasions, usually works better than a constant stream that dilutes the sense of occasion each one carries.

Is it fine to bring back a popular limited beer?

Occasionally, and clearly labelled as a return rather than pretending it never happened, but bringing it back too often turns a limited beer into a regular one in every way except the name.

What makes a seasonal beer feel authentic rather than manufactured?

A real constraint behind it, whether that is a short ingredient season, a small batch size, or a genuine one-off recipe, rather than a marketing label applied to an otherwise ordinary beer.
