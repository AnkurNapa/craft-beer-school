Branded house or house of brands: naming what your brewery makes next

Sooner or later every brewery makes something that is not quite its core beer. Before you design a label for it, decide how it sits next to the brewery name. This guide walks through the two models and the two questions that choose between them.

Every new product forces a naming decision

A brewpub with six core beers on tap rarely thinks about brand architecture. Then someone proposes a hard seltzer for summer, or a hop water for the drivers in the group. Then the question arrives: does it carry the brewery name or one of its own?

Treat it as a core step of brand strategy, next to positioning and values. The choice you make for product number seven shapes how you name products eight to twenty. It also decides how much of your marketing money builds one name rather than several. It is one stage of the bigger process in our guide to rebranding a brewery.

A branded house puts your name on everything

In a branded house the brewery is the hero. Every beer, seltzer, T-shirt and food menu leans on the parent name. Drinkers who like your pale ale will try the new 330 ml can because they already trust the name printed at the top.

The upside is focus. One Instagram handle, one logo system and one story carry every product. The risk is that a weak launch rubs off on the beers that pay the bills. A badly received sour or a flat seltzer is now a story about your brewery, not about one product.

A house of brands lets each product stand alone

In a house of brands the parent steps back, sometimes out of sight. Each product gets its own name, look and audience. This suits a product aimed at people who would never walk into your taproom, or one whose personality clashes with your core beers.

The cost is real. Two brands means two sets of design work, two social accounts to feed and two names to check for trademark conflicts. In India it can also mean a fresh label approval round for each name in every state you sell in. Budget 5 to 15% of a year's revenue for a full rebrand, less for a refresh. On a ₹2 crore turnover that is ₹10 lakh to ₹30 lakh (2 crore x 0.05 and x 0.15). A second brand roughly doubles the design bill. One brand done well beats three done thinly.

There is a middle path that many breweries drift into: a product with its own name plus a small line that says who brews it. It works, but choose it on purpose rather than by accident.

Two yes or no questions settle most of it

Put two questions to the team:

  1. Will the brewery name go on every product you make, beer or not?
  2. Will you ever launch a product under a completely new name?

A yes to the first points to a branded house. A yes to the second points to a house of brands. Two yeses usually mean the team has not yet agreed on what the brewery is for, which is worth knowing before you brief a designer. A neutral outsider who has run brand portfolios before can often settle that argument in an hour. Our guide on how beer companies build a brand portfolio shows how the large players give each brand a single job.

Line extensions test the choice fastest

Here are four extensions an Indian brewery might reasonably add. Each pulls on the decision in its own way:

  • Hard seltzer. It often appeals to people who say they do not like beer. That argues for its own name, so your IPA drinkers are not confused and seltzer drinkers are not put off.
  • Hop water. A zero alcohol drink made with hops sits close to your craft. The brewery name can lend it credibility. A non-alcoholic product may also fall under food rules rather than excise, so check how it is regulated before you assume one label process covers both.
  • RTD drinks. A canned cocktail or spritz is a different category with different drinkers. Many breweries give these a separate name.
  • A food concept. A kitchen brand that delivers through apps may want to stand alone, partly because a brewery name on a delivery listing can raise questions under alcohol advertising rules.

None of these answers is fixed. The point is to ask the two questions for each one before anyone opens a design file.

One 45 minute meeting settles the pattern

Gather the four to six people who will sign off the brand. Give the session 45 minutes. List every product you might launch in the next three years, including the ones that are only bar talk. For each, ask the two questions and write down the answer next to it.

Then look at the pattern. If eight of ten products carry the brewery name, you are a branded house and the outliers need a good reason to leave. If the list splits evenly, name that tension and settle it now. Settling it after printing 10,000 cans costs far more. When a product earns its own name, our guide to naming a beer brand picks up from there.

Your first step: put that three-year product list on one page today and share it with the team before the meeting.

Common questions

What is a branded house in brewing?

A brand structure where the brewery name appears on every product, so each beer or extension borrows trust from the parent name.

What is a house of brands?

A structure where products carry their own names and the parent brewery stays in the background or out of sight.

Should a hard seltzer carry the brewery name?

It depends on who it is for. If it targets people who do not drink beer, a separate name often works better. Ask the two questions in this guide first.

Does a new brand name need new label approval in India?

Usually a new label means a fresh approval in each state where it is sold. Rules vary by state and change, so check the current notification or ask a licensing consultant.
