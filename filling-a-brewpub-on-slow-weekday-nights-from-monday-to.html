Filling a brewpub on slow weekday nights from Monday to Thursday

Most brewpubs are full on Friday and Saturday and echoing on Tuesday. The fixed costs are the same every night, so even a modest lift in weekday covers changes the month. Here is how we would approach it.

4 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

Why weekdays matter so much

Rent, salaries, the glycol chiller and the brewhouse loan do not care what day it is. A brewpub that only fills two nights a week carries its fixed costs on those two nights. Every extra weekday table is close to pure contribution, because the staff and the beer are already there.

Beer freshness is part of it too. A tank of hoppy beer has a window. If weekday sales are thin, that beer is still on tap three weeks later and not at its best. Our guide to taproom economics shows how much the quiet nights weigh on the month.

Start with your numbers, not ideas

Pull covers and beer litres by day of the week and by hour for the last three months from your POS. Look for the real dead zones. Often Monday is hopeless, Tuesday and Wednesday are quiet after 9 PM and Thursday is already half a weekend. Each needs a different answer.

Then ask who is within a short drive on a weeknight: offices, colleges, apartment complexes, hotels. A brewpub in a tech corridor has a different weekday crowd from one in a residential area.

Regular nights people can plan around

Weekday events work when they are regular. A quiz every Wednesday builds a crowd over months. A one-off event every few weeks rarely does. Pick one or two anchor nights and commit to them for at least a quarter before judging.

  • Quiz nights, run by someone good at it, with teams that come back weekly.
  • Brewer's table, where the head brewer pours the tank-fresh beer, explains it and takes questions.
  • Live sport on the nights cricket or football fixtures fall, with seating that suits watching.
  • Board games or open mic nights for a younger crowd who stay longer and drink more slowly.

Food that gives people a reason

A weekday visit is often a dinner decision, not a drinking one. A set meal with a beer pairing, a regional food night or a reasonably priced thali-style platter can bring in couples and families who would never come for a party night. Pairing beers with real dishes from your city, explained by staff who have tasted them, makes the beer part of the meal.

Offers, carefully

Happy hours and weekday prices can work, but they are paid for in margin and they train people to come only when it is cheap. Check what your state's excise rules allow on liquor promotions before you print anything. Where they are allowed, tie offers to something you want more of, such as early covers, a new beer or growler fills, rather than discounting everything.

Promote responsibly. A weekday offer should bring people in for an evening, not encourage heavy drinking on a work night.

Groups and offices

Weekday evenings suit team dinners, farewells and small office celebrations. Make it easy to book: a simple group menu, a set price per head, a corner that can be reserved and a named person who answers the phone. Visit nearby offices with a menu and a tasting invitation. A few regular corporate bookings a month can fill nights no event will.

Brewery tours before dinner work well for groups too, especially for people who have never seen a brewhouse.

Use the beer itself

A brewpub has something no ordinary bar has: beer made a few metres away. Use the weekday calendar for small releases. Tap a one-off keg on a Wednesday, announce it to regulars that morning and let the brewer pour the first glasses. A small-batch beer that is only on for a few nights gives people a reason to come midweek rather than wait for Saturday.

Weekday evenings are also the right time for a slower, guided tasting flight with notes, for guests who want to learn rather than party. Those guests often become the regulars who bring friends at the weekend.

Build the community, then measure

Regulars fill weekdays. A mug club, a WhatsApp group for new beer announcements or members-only tastings give people a reason to come on a Tuesday. Our guide to building a beer community goes deeper.

Track each idea against the baseline you pulled at the start: covers, litres and spend per head on that night. Give it a quarter. Drop what does not move the numbers and double what does. Most brewpubs try ideas at random. The ones that fill weekdays run it like a small experiment. Knowing how to plan, promote and measure those experiments is what turns a quiet Tuesday into a habit.

Common questions

What is the best event for a slow weekday?

There is no single answer, but regular, weekly events such as quiz nights or a brewer's table build crowds better than occasional one-offs.

Should I run happy hours?

They can help if your state allows them, but they cost margin. Tie offers to things you want more of and promote them responsibly.

How long should I try a new weekday idea?

Give it at least a quarter and compare covers, litres and spend per head against the same night before you started.

Do corporate bookings help?

Yes. Simple group menus, set prices and an easy booking contact can fill weekday evenings with team dinners and celebrations.
