Working with beer creators

Working with creators can genuinely extend a brewery's reach, but only with the right person, a clear ask, and an honest understanding of what Indian rules allow.

3 min read · Updated August 2024 · Craft Beer School

On this page

  1. Audience match matters more than follower count
  2. Be specific about what you are asking for
  3. Disclosure is not optional
  4. Age gating applies to influencer content too
  5. Pay fairly, or be clear that you cannot
  6. Measure what actually matters

Audience match matters more than follower count

A creator with a smaller but genuinely engaged audience of people who already drink craft beer is worth more to a brewery than a larger account whose followers have no particular interest in beer, however impressive the number looks in a proposal deck. Look at the comments on a creator's recent posts before reaching out, not just the follower number on their profile, because comments reveal whether people actually engage or simply scroll past without registering the brand name at all.

A food or travel creator based in your own city, with an audience that overlaps with your taproom's actual catchment area, is often a better fit than a much larger national account with no local relevance to where your beer is actually sold, because that reach rarely converts into anyone actually walking through the taproom door. A quick scroll through where the creator's own followers are located, when the platform shows it, is worth more than any headline number sitting at the top of their profile.

Be specific about what you are asking for

A vague brief that just says post about our beer produces vague, forgettable content. Ask for something specific: a short video of a tasting, a written comparison against a style the creator already knows, coverage of a particular event at the taproom, or a short series across a launch week rather than a single isolated post that disappears from a feed within a day. Specificity gives the creator something to work with and gives you content you can actually reuse on your own channels afterwards, which is often worth more to a small brewery over time than the original post's reach ever was.

Disclosure is not optional

Any paid partnership or free product arrangement needs to be disclosed clearly, both under platform rules and under general advertising standards, and alcohol content adds an extra layer of scrutiny given the restrictions covered in our advertising rules guide. Agree on disclosure wording before content goes live, not after a platform or regulator flags it, because a retracted post rarely undoes the impression the original one already left behind.

Age gating applies to influencer content too

Content produced in partnership with a creator is still alcohol marketing in the eyes of a regulator, and the same baseline expectations around age gating and avoiding language that encourages consumption apply just as they would to content posted directly from the brewery's own account, regardless of who filmed it or whose name is on the post, and the brewery, not the creator, usually carries the final responsibility under its own licence. A creator unfamiliar with these restrictions, particularly one used to working with categories that face no such limits, may need a short, clear brief covering what they can and cannot show or say on camera.

Pay fairly, or be clear that you cannot

Expecting a creator with a real audience to work purely for free product undervalues both their time and the trust they have built with their audience over months or years, and it tends to produce lower effort content in return, and it signals to a creator that the brand does not value their audience the way it values its own beer, a signal that tends to travel through a small creator community faster than any brief intended. If budget genuinely does not allow payment, be upfront about that rather than framing a small amount of free beer as equivalent compensation, and expect a correspondingly smaller ask in return, matched honestly to what is actually being offered.

Measure what actually matters

Follower growth on your own account is a weak, easily gamed signal of whether a partnership actually worked. Track whether it drove people to a specific event, a specific release, or a measurable bump in taproom visits during the week the content went live, which ties directly back to the kind of planning covered in our social media guide and taproom events guide. A partnership that produced a lot of likes but no visible change in either is not one worth repeating in the same form.

Common questions

Do creators need to disclose free beer they received?

Yes. Any paid partnership or free product arrangement needs clear disclosure under platform rules and general advertising standards, and this applies to alcohol content as much as any other category.

Should we work with the biggest beer influencer we can find?

Not necessarily. A smaller, genuinely engaged local audience often produces better results for a brewery than a large but poorly matched one.

Can influencer content include people drinking on camera?

Rules and enforcement vary by state and platform, so confirm with your excise department before a shoot rather than assuming a format used elsewhere in India is automatically safe for your licence.
