Beer label rules in India

Beer labels in India answer to two authorities at once: FSSAI for food labelling standards, and each state's excise department for its own approval process. Here is what that generally means for a founder designing one.

3 min read · Updated April 2022 · By Chatty Girija

Written byChatty GirijaBeer Podcaster · Creative Strategist

  2. What generally has to appear
  3. The approval process, in general shape
  4. Designing with approval in mind
  5. The rules will not stay still

Two sets of requirements, applied together

FSSAI sets baseline food labelling requirements that apply to beer as a packaged food product: net quantity, manufacturer or marketer details, batch identification and any FSSAI-mandated declarations that apply to beverages generally. Separately, each state's excise department typically requires its own label approval before a brand can be sold in that state, checking things like alcohol content declaration, statutory warning text and placement, and sometimes state-specific formatting rules on top.

A label approved in one state is not automatically approved in the next, which surprises founders expanding for the first time, and it is a large part of why a phased, state-by-state expansion plan generally works better than an ambitious multi-state launch attempted all at once with a single, unproven label design.

What generally has to appear

Expect to include: the brand and product name, net volume, alcohol by volume, manufacturer or brewer details, a batch or lot identifier for traceability, and the statutory health warning text required for alcoholic beverages sold in India. Exact wording, required font size and placement of the warning vary by state, so do not assume the same warning text and layout that works in one state will pass in another without checking first, however minor the difference looks on paper when you first compare the two notifications side by side.

The approval process, in general shape

Most states require label approval as a separate step from the manufacturing licence itself, often needing physical or digital submission of the artwork to the excise department before the brand can be sold there legally. Processing time varies by state and is rarely instant, so factor label approval into your launch timeline as its own milestone, not an assumption bundled into general licensing that might slip.

This connects to excise duty on beer in India, since label approval and duty registration often move through related, but not identical, state processes running in parallel.

Designing with approval in mind

Build required text and its minimum size into your label layout from the first design draft, covered further in beer label design that works, rather than treating compliance text as something to squeeze in once the creative work is approved internally by the team. A label that needs significant rework to pass state approval delays a launch far more than a slightly less striking design that clears on the first submission.

The rules will not stay still

Label content requirements, approval processes and fee structures vary by state and are updated periodically, sometimes with limited public notice given to brewers already mid-design. Before finalising any label for print, check the current requirement with the relevant state excise department or a licensing consultant, rather than relying on what a label approved a year or two ago required at the time.

A common and costly mistake is printing a large run of labels before the final approval comes through. State excise departments sometimes ask for a small wording change late in the process, and a warehouse full of the wrong labels is an expensive lesson. Order a short trial run first and confirm the approved artwork before committing to volume.

Planning around a multi-state launch

If you plan to sell in more than one state within the first year, get each state's label requirements confirmed before finalising a single master design, rather than designing once for your home state and reworking it later under time pressure. Some brands design a flexible base layout with a clearly marked zone for the variable, state-specific text, warning wording, local distributor details, so a design update for a new state means editing that zone rather than rebuilding the label from scratch.

This costs a little more design discipline up front and saves a great deal of rushed rework later, once a second or third state is genuinely on the table.

Keep a simple tracker of which version of the label is approved in which state and when it needs renewal, since managing several approved variants without a clear record is a fast route to accidentally shipping the wrong label to the wrong state.

A shipment held up because the wrong variant of a label reached the wrong warehouse is an entirely avoidable delay, and one that a basic spreadsheet, checked before every despatch, prevents at almost no cost, however unglamorous that spreadsheet is to maintain.

Common questions

Does one label design work across all Indian states?

Not automatically. Each state typically requires its own label approval, and requirements for warning text, placement and format can differ by state.

What information must appear on a beer label in India?

Generally: brand and product name, net volume, ABV, manufacturer details, a batch identifier, and the statutory health warning text, though exact requirements vary by state.

Is FSSAI labelling separate from state excise label approval?

Yes. FSSAI sets baseline food labelling standards, while each state's excise department separately approves the label for sale within that state.

How long does label approval take?

It varies by state and is not usually instant, so treat it as its own milestone in your launch timeline rather than assuming it happens alongside general licensing.
