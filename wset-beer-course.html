WSET Level 1 Award in Beer

The WSET Level 1 Award in Beer is a compact, structured introduction to tasting and describing beer properly. Here is what it actually trains and who it is built for.

A systematic approach to tasting, borrowed from wine

WSET built its reputation training people to taste wine with a consistent, repeatable method, breaking a glass down into appearance, nose, palate and conclusion in a way that produces comparable notes from one taster to the next, rather than a pile of personal impressions that mean nothing to anyone else. The beer qualification applies the same discipline to beer, teaching a vocabulary and a structure for describing a glass that holds up whether the taster is judging a stout or a pale lager, and whether the taster tried the beer this week or six months ago.

This systematic habit is worth more long after the exam than the qualification itself, because it is the actual skill that separates a trained taster from an enthusiastic one. Anyone can say a beer tastes good or bad. Far fewer people can explain, in specific and repeatable terms, exactly why, and that specificity is what the method actually trains into a taster over time, whether they end up working behind a bar, judging a competition, or simply enjoying beer more deliberately.

Level 1 is an introduction, not a deep specialism

The entry level award covers the main beer styles, basic production knowledge, food pairing principles, basic serving temperature and glassware considerations, and the structured tasting method itself, aimed at people who are relatively new to formal beer education rather than seasoned professionals. It suits a hospitality worker, a retailer, a homebrewer, or simply a serious enthusiast who wants a proper foundation before going further, rather than someone already deep into judging or brewing. It works well as a first formal step precisely because it does not assume any prior study, which makes it a sensible starting point before attempting Cicerone or BJCP.

A single day or weekend format is common for the Level 1 award, which suits someone testing the waters before committing to a longer study path, and the exam itself is generally shorter and less demanding than the higher WSET levels built for wine and spirits.

How it sits next to Cicerone and BJCP

Where Cicerone leans toward service and draught knowledge and BJCP leans toward competition judging against a fixed style guideline, WSET leans toward formal, structured tasting as a transferable, general purpose skill, one that also applies directly to wine and spirits if a career moves in that direction later, which gives it a portability the other two beer-specific credentials do not offer in quite the same way, a genuine practical advantage for anyone whose interest in drinks extends beyond beer alone. Our certifications comparison guide sets all three side by side if you are still deciding which one to start with first.

Check the current syllabus and fees directly with WSET

Course content, exam format and fees for the Level 1 Award in Beer are set and updated by WSET itself, and they are the only reliable source for current details, since third-party summaries, including this one, can lag behind an actual syllabus change, and a candidate studying from an outdated guide risks preparing for an exam that no longer matches what is actually asked of them on the day.

Where to study it from India

WSET courses run through a network of approved providers, some of which operate in India and some of which teach internationally over structured remote formats. Confirm which delivery format an approved provider actually offers before enrolling, since not every course runs in every city or in every session, and online and in-person formats can differ meaningfully in how much hands-on tasting guidance a candidate actually receives during the course itself.

Building on it afterwards

A WSET Level 1 award is a genuinely useful foundation before attempting BJCP judging, covered in our BJCP exam guide, because the structured tasting habit it builds transfers directly into the more demanding style-specific judging BJCP requires of its certified judges, giving a candidate a head start on the discipline rather than starting that habit from nothing.

Whichever certification path you choose, the qualification on its own is worth less than the tasting discipline behind it. Keep a simple notebook of every beer you assess against a set format, noting appearance, aroma, flavour and finish in the same order every time. That habit matters more on the job than the certificate on the wall.

Common questions

Is WSET only for wine?

No. WSET runs separate, dedicated qualifications for beer and spirits alongside its well known wine programme, each with its own syllabus and exam.

Do I need any beer knowledge before starting Level 1?

No prior formal knowledge is required. It is designed as an entry point, though a genuine interest in tasting carefully helps considerably.

Is WSET recognised by Indian employers?

Recognition varies by employer and role, but the structured tasting skill it builds is broadly useful across hospitality, retail and beverage roles, regardless of whether a specific employer names it directly.
