Strong beer and mild beer in India: why the split matters to teams

Indian beer is sold in two broad strength classes: mild and strong. The split shapes tax, recipes, pricing and brand strategy. If your work touches beer data or beer brands, treating the two as one category will mislead you.

3 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

Where the line sits

In common Indian usage, mild beer is beer up to about 5% alcohol by volume. Strong beer is above that, usually up to about 8%, which is the upper limit many people associate with beer in India. The exact thresholds, names and tax treatment are set by each state and can change, so always check the current state excise notification before relying on a figure.

The labels tell you which is which. Strong beers usually say so prominently on the bottle or can. The declared ABV is printed on every pack.

Strong beer has long made up the larger share of beer volume in India. That is unusual. In many other big beer markets the bulk of the volume is standard lager at around 4 to 5%.

Why it matters for tax and price

Many states tax beer differently by strength, either through separate duty rates for mild and strong or through duty linked to alcohol content. The result is that a strong and a mild beer from the same brewer can carry quite different shelf prices. The gap varies from state to state. Our guide to beer excise duty in India explains the general mechanics.

For pricing analysts this means comparisons must be made within a strength class and within a state. Comparing a strong beer in one state with a mild beer in another tells you almost nothing about brand value.

How strong beer is made differently

More alcohol needs more fermentable sugar. Brewers get there in a few ways: more malt, more adjuncts such as rice, maize or brewing sugars and a fermentation pushed further. Large breweries commonly brew a concentrated beer and dilute it with treated, deaerated water to the final strength. This is called high gravity brewing. It lets one brew produce more finished beer, which saves tank time. Our guide to high gravity brewing and dilution covers it in more detail.

Strong beer is harder to make well. Yeast under the stress of high sugar and alcohol can throw off solvent-like fusel alcohols, sulphur and other off flavours. Fermentation needs careful temperature control and healthy yeast. A good strong lager tastes smooth and rounded. A poor one tastes hot and harsh. Drinkers notice.

On the brewhouse floor the difference shows up as a number: a strong lager at sales strength might start from around 16 to 18 °P of extract, where a mild lager needs closer to 10 or 11 °P. High gravity brews go higher still before dilution.

Two categories with different drinkers

Strong and mild beer often attract different occasions and different buyers. Strong beer is frequently chosen on alcohol per rupee. Mild beer leans towards refreshment and long sessions with food. Craft beers in India sit mostly in the 4.5 to 7% range and cut across both classes. Occasion data helps here: a sports night with friends and a quiet dinner at home can favour different strengths from the same drinker.

For portfolio teams, the split means a brand family often needs both: a strong variant and a mild one, sometimes under the same name. See beer brand portfolios explained for how brewers structure that.

What changes in the data

  • Separate categories. Track strong and mild as separate segments in sales and share reports. Mixing them hides real shifts.
  • Alcohol as a unit. Some teams also report litres of pure alcohol, which makes strong and mild comparable on alcohol content. It is useful for tax analysis but should not replace litres of beer.
  • Policy events. A change in how one state taxes strong beer can shift volume between classes quickly. Mark those dates on every chart.
  • Cost of goods. Strong beer uses more raw material per litre. Compare costs within a class, not across.

A tasting that explains the split

The quickest way for a team to understand the difference is a small guided tasting of a mild lager and a strong lager side by side, with a brewer explaining what they are tasting. Keep it opt-in and within company policy, with small pours, water, spit cups and room for colleagues who prefer not to drink to smell and discuss. People come out knowing why the two categories behave differently in the market.

Common questions

What ABV counts as strong beer in India?

Commonly above about 5% ABV, up to about 8%. Thresholds and tax treatment are set by each state and change, so check the current notification.

Why is strong beer so popular in India?

Many drinkers choose it for alcohol per rupee. It has also been the main mainstream offer for decades. Taste, price and habit all play a part.

Is strong beer made by adding alcohol?

No. The alcohol comes from fermentation. Brewers use more fermentable sugar from malt and adjuncts and often brew at high gravity, then dilute to the declared strength.
