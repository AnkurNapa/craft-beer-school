Burnout in brewing: spotting it before you lose a good brewer

Brewing attracts people who love the work. That love is easy to spend. When a good brewer burns out you lose their skill, months of hiring and often a few batches on the way. Most of the causes sit in the rota, not in the person.

The brewer who never complains is the one to watch

Burnout rarely announces itself. The brewer still arrives at 6 am for a brew day and still hits the gravity. What changes is quieter: shorter answers, no new recipe ideas, a flat "fine" when you ask about the IPA they used to fuss over.

Researchers usually describe burnout in three parts: exhaustion, a growing distance or cynicism about the work and a sense that nothing you do makes a difference. A US study of 262 brewery and distillery workers, reported in The New Brewer, the Brewers Association's journal, found the exhaustion clearly but found people still felt capable and proud of their work. That is a US finding. Its point travels: the people are not failing, the system around them is.

Count the hours a brewing week really takes

Add it up for a single head brewer on a 10 hl brewhouse brewing four times a week. A brew day with mashing, boil, transfer and cleaning is often 9 to 10 hours. That is 4 x 10 = 40 hours before a single keg is filled, a tank is dry hopped, a yeast count is done or a delivery problem is answered.

Add cellar work, packaging and a six-day week, which is common in Indian breweries. A 55 to 60 hour week becomes normal. Then the taproom wants the brewer to host a tasting on Friday night. None of these tasks is unreasonable. Together they leave no recovery time, week after week, through a summer when the brewhouse floor is hot well before noon.

Signs worth noticing early

None of these proves anything on its own. Three or four together, lasting more than a couple of weeks, are worth a conversation:

  • Small mistakes from someone who never made them: a missed hop addition, a valve left open.
  • Withdrawal from the team, skipping the shift meal or the tasting panel.
  • Irritation at things that used to be jokes.
  • Leave not taken, or taken and spent answering messages about the fermenter.
  • Talk of "just getting through" the month, every month.

Watch for changes in drinking habits too. A brewery makes alcohol easy to reach at the end of a long shift. A post-shift beer that becomes several is a reason to talk, kindly and privately.

The fixes are mostly in the rota

Owners control most of the causes. Practical changes that cost little:

  1. Cross-train a second person on brew days so the head brewer can take a real day off. One person who can run a mash and a CIP safely changes everything.
  2. Cap brew days per person per week. Protect at least one full day off with no messages.
  3. Separate the brewer from late taproom duty, or make it a paid, occasional choice.
  4. Plan packaging so it does not land on the same day as a double brew.
  5. Hold a short one-to-one every month that is not about beer.

What losing a good brewer actually costs

Work it through roughly. Hiring an experienced brewer in India can take two to three months. A new hire may need another two or three to learn your kit and recipes. Call it five months of the owner or an assistant covering the gap.

During handovers, batches go wrong. If even one 1,000 litre batch is dumped, multiply that by your own cost per litre. Add the recipes and the feel for your system that walk out the door. A second brewer's part-time wage to share the load is usually the cheaper option. Seeing that in your own numbers is a management skill, not a soft one.

When it is more than tiredness

An owner is not a counsellor and should not try to be one. If someone describes feeling hopeless for weeks, cannot sleep or talks about harming themselves, encourage them to speak to a doctor or a mental health professional. Help them find the time to go. India's national Tele MANAS helpline (14416) offers free support. In an emergency, call 112.

The same applies to owners. Founders routinely work the longest hours in the building and are the last to admit it.

A first step for Monday

Write down every hour each person on your brewing team actually worked last week, including messages after hours. Put the numbers on one page, look for the person with the highest total and ask them, privately, what one task you could take off them this month. Then take it off them.

Common questions

Is burnout the same as being tired?

No. Tiredness passes with rest. Burnout is usually described as exhaustion plus a growing distance from the work and a feeling that effort makes no difference. It builds over months.

What can a small brewery owner change quickly?

Cross-train a second person on brew days, protect a full day off each week and keep brewers off late taproom shifts unless they choose them.

When should someone get professional help?

If low mood, poor sleep or hopelessness lasts for weeks, or there is any thought of self-harm, they should see a doctor or mental health professional. In India, Tele MANAS on 14416 is a free starting point.
