Brewery or distillery: comparing the two career paths

Brewing and distilling share a first step and then go very different ways. Here is what each job looks like day to day in India, what carries over and how to decide which one to chase.

3 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

The same start, a different finish

A brewer and a distiller both turn starch into sugar and sugar into alcohol with yeast. The brewer stops there, conditions the beer and sends it out within weeks. The distiller takes that fermented liquid, called the wash. Then they concentrate the alcohol by heating it in a still. For whisky the spirit then goes into oak casks and waits for years.

That one difference shapes everything about the job. A brewer gets feedback on a decision in a fortnight. A distiller making whisky may not taste the full result of a cut decision until long after the batch has been forgotten by everyone else. If you want the basics of the process first, our guide to how whisky is made walks through it.

What a working day looks like

In a brewery or brewpub the day is built around brew days, cellar checks and cleaning. You mash in, run off the wort, boil it, cool it and pitch yeast. Then you spend a surprising share of your hours on cleaning in place, checking gravities, dry hopping and kegging. Brewpub teams in India are small, so the head brewer often runs the filter, fixes the pump seal and talks to the bar manager in the same shift.

In a distillery the work is more continuous. Stills may run in shifts round the clock. A malt whisky line ferments a wash of around 8% ABV, runs it through a wash still to make low wines in the low twenties, then redistils that in a spirit still. The stillman decides where to cut between heads, hearts and tails. Hearts come off as new make spirit at roughly 70% ABV. Every litre is logged because spirit under bond is watched closely by excise.

Pace, scale and feedback

Breweries reward people who like quick loops. You change a hop schedule, taste the result in three weeks and adjust. Distilleries reward patience and record keeping. A cask filled today is a decision someone else may judge years from now.

Scale differs too. Many Indian distilleries are large plants making neutral spirit from molasses for blended brands, with craft malt and gin makers forming a smaller group. Breweries range from big lager plants to brewpubs with a 5 or 10 hectolitre kit. In a brewpub you will touch every step. In a large distillery you may own one section of the process for a long time.

Where the jobs are in India

Brewing jobs cluster where brewpubs are allowed and busy: Bengaluru, Pune, Mumbai, Gurugram and Hyderabad, plus the large brewery plants that sit outside cities. Our guide to jobs in a brewery lists the roles in order.

Distillery jobs follow raw material and licences. Molasses based plants tend to sit near sugar producing regions. Craft gin and single malt producers have grown in the last decade, with Goa a common base for small gin makers. Rules on who can distil and where vary by state and change, so check the current position before you plan a move.

What carries over and what does not

  • Carries over: fermentation science, yeast health, hygiene, sensory skills, reading a gravity or ABV and keeping a clean batch record.
  • Brewing only: hop chemistry, carbonation, packaging and the cold chain for a fresh product.
  • Distilling only: still operation, cut points, cask management, warehouse stock control and the safety discipline of working with flammable spirit vapour.

A good brewer can learn to run a still. What usually takes longer is the mindset: the distillery cares about consistency over years, the brewpub cares about this week's tap list.

How to choose

Ask yourself four honest questions. Do you want to see results fast or are you happy to wait? Do you want to do everything yourself or own one process well? Would you rather live in a city near brewpubs or near a plant? Are you comfortable with shift work round the clock?

Then go and stand in both places. Ask for a visit or a trial day at a brewpub and a distillery. Most people know within a few hours which noise, smell and rhythm suits them. What a visit will not teach you is why a cut point moves or why a cask behaves the way it does. That is the gap a structured course fills before you hand in your notice.

Common questions

Can a brewer move into a distillery later?

Yes. The fermentation and hygiene skills transfer directly. You would need to learn still operation, cuts and cask management, which most distilleries teach on the job alongside formal training.

Is whisky in India aged for three years like Scotch?

The three year minimum in oak is a Scotch whisky rule. Indian rules are set separately and vary. Indian heat speeds up maturation while raising evaporation loss well above Scotland's.

Which job involves more shift work?

Usually the distillery, because stills and fermenters in larger plants run round the clock. Brewpubs have long days around brew day and service but fewer night shifts.
