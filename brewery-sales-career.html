A career in brewery sales

Brewery sales is one of the least understood entry points into the beer industry, and one of the most accessible, because it rewards relationship skills and product knowledge over technical brewing experience.

3 min read · Updated September 2023 · Craft Beer School

What the job actually covers

A brewery sales role involves winning new accounts, on-premise (bars and restaurants) or off-premise (retail), managing existing relationships, negotiating placement and pricing within the brewery's guidelines, and coordinating with distributors where the brewery does not sell direct. It is closer to a general field sales job than to anything happening on the brewhouse floor.

A meaningful part of the role is also logistics: making sure the right beer arrives at the right account in good condition and on time, and following up when a tap line or a delivery has gone wrong. Sales in this industry does not stop at the signed order, it continues through every delivery afterwards.

Why product knowledge still matters here

A salesperson who can describe a beer accurately, its style, its strength, why it pairs with a given dish, wins trust with an account faster than one reciting a generic pitch. This is where sensory training becomes genuinely useful for a sales career, not just a brewing one, because it gives you the vocabulary to talk about the product credibly.

An account owner who has been pitched a dozen new beers that month can tell within a minute whether the person in front of them actually understands what they are selling. That difference alone often decides whether a new listing gets tried at all.

Understanding the account's economics, not just your own

A bar or retailer is thinking about margin, shelf space and how fast a keg or case will turn over, not just whether the beer tastes good. A salesperson who understands margin and turnover from the buyer's side of the table can build a pitch that actually addresses what the account cares about, rather than talking only about the brewery's story.

A slow-turning keg ties up an account's cold storage and cash, which is exactly the kind of concern a good salesperson raises before the buyer has to. Coming prepared with a realistic estimate of how fast a given beer usually moves, rather than an optimistic one, builds far more trust over time.

Route to market and the distribution layer

In India, many breweries sell partly direct and partly through distributors, and the mix varies heavily by state and by how the local three-tier or state-controlled system is structured, which is worth understanding in general terms even though the exact rules differ by state and change over time. A sales role often means managing the relationship on both sides of that split.

A salesperson working across several states needs to hold two things at once: a consistent pitch about the beer, and a genuinely different understanding of the route to market, paperwork and timelines in each state. Confirming the current process with the brewery's own licensing or compliance contact before committing to a promise is a habit worth building early.

What makes someone good at this job

Reliability, follow-through and being straightforward about what a brewery can and cannot deliver, on timelines, on volume, on new releases, build long-term account relationships faster than aggressive pitching. Beer sales is a repeat business, not a one-time close, and accounts remember who kept their word.

Overpromising is the fastest way to lose an account permanently. If a brewery cannot guarantee weekly delivery of a seasonal beer, say so up front rather than letting an account discover it after they have already put it on the menu. A missed promise on a small order is remembered far longer than the order itself.

Getting into brewery sales without a brewing background

General sales or hospitality experience is often enough to get considered for an entry-level brewery sales role, and pairing that with a short course that builds real product and process knowledge closes the credibility gap quickly. It is one of the more direct paths into the beer industry for someone coming from outside it entirely.

Many people already working in FMCG or hospitality distribution move into brewery sales because the core skills, cold outreach, account management, negotiation, transfer directly. What they usually need is the beer-specific knowledge that lets them speak confidently to a bar owner or retail buyer about the product itself, which is exactly the gap a short course closes fastest.

Common questions

Do I need brewing knowledge to work in brewery sales?

Not strictly, but understanding the product well enough to describe it accurately makes you far more effective with accounts than a generic pitch ever will.

Is brewery sales a good entry point without industry experience?

Yes. General sales or hospitality experience combined with some product training is often enough to be considered for entry-level roles.

How does distribution work for Indian breweries?

It varies by state, with some breweries selling partly direct and partly through distributors depending on the local regulatory structure. Rules differ by state and change, so check current requirements directly.

What skill matters most for long-term success in this role?

Reliability and follow-through with accounts. Brewery sales is a repeat relationship business, not a series of one-time pitches.
