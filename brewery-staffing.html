Staffing a small brewery

Staffing decisions in a small brewery are really role decisions: what needs doing, and which of those things one competent person can genuinely combine. Here is how to think about the early team.

3 min read · Updated January 2025 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

  2. Your first brewing hire
  3. Cellar and packaging staff
  4. Sales, compliance and the parts founders often do themselves
  5. Training and retention in a young industry
  6. Structuring pay and progression early

The roles that must exist, whoever fills them

Every brewery, however small, needs someone responsible for brewing and quality control, someone responsible for cleaning and cellar work, someone handling sales and account relationships if you sell beyond your own taproom, and someone tracking compliance, licensing and basic finance. In a very small operation, one person can genuinely hold two or three of these, but the responsibilities themselves do not disappear just because nobody is formally assigned to them.

Map these functions out explicitly before you hire, even if the initial answer is that the founder covers three of them personally for the first several months.

Your first brewing hire

Look for someone who understands process discipline, temperature control, sanitation, record keeping, over someone who only knows recipe formulation. A brewery lives or dies on consistency far more than on creativity, and a brewer who logs every batch accurately and notices when a fermentation is running warm is worth more early on than one chasing an interesting new style every month.

Formal brewing qualifications help but are not universal in India yet; hands-on experience and a demonstrable feel for process discipline often matter more at interview than a certificate, particularly given how young the formal training ecosystem still is.

Cellar and packaging staff

Cleaning, transfers, kegging or canning and general cellar work are physically demanding and unglamorous, and they are also where most contamination and quality problems actually originate if done carelessly, regardless of how well the brewing itself was executed. Train for this role deliberately rather than assuming anyone can pick it up, because a poorly cleaned line or an under-sanitised keg costs you a customer's trust the moment they taste it, and that trust is expensive to rebuild.

Sales, compliance and the parts founders often do themselves

In the earliest stage, the founder frequently ends up doing sales calls and compliance filing personally, and that is a reasonable way to start, since nobody understands the business better yet. The transition point comes when compliance deadlines start slipping because the founder is out selling, or sales calls stop happening because the founder is buried in an excise renewal.

That is the signal to hire, not a fixed headcount target set in advance. See a brewery compliance checklist for the workload this role actually carries once you look at it in full.

Training and retention in a young industry

Craft brewing is still a young trade in India, and experienced staff, especially trained brewers and cellar leads, are genuinely scarce and get poached between breweries competing for the same small pool of people. Invest in training your own people early, document your processes so knowledge does not walk out the door with one departure, and treat retention as a real cost line, not an afterthought squeezed in once everything else is budgeted.

A brewery that trains well but loses staff often still comes out ahead of one that never trains at all, because the documentation and discipline built along the way outlast any one individual.

Structuring pay and progression early

Even a small team benefits from a clear, if simple, sense of how pay and responsibility grow, since a cellar hand who cannot see a path toward a brewing role has little reason to stay once a competing brewery offers the same wage for the same work. Tie progression to demonstrated skill, running a clean-in-place cycle unsupervised, reading and correcting a stuck mash, rather than to tenure alone, since skill is what actually protects your beer quality as the team grows.

This need not be a formal HR framework in a five-person operation, but writing down what each level of responsibility actually looks like avoids the awkward, ad hoc negotiations that otherwise happen every time someone feels underpaid.

It also gives you a fairer basis for those conversations, since both sides can point to the same written expectation rather than relying on memory or goodwill alone.

Revisit the structure at least once a year as the brewery grows, since roles and responsibilities that made sense for a five-person team rarely still fit cleanly once headcount doubles, and a structure left unreviewed tends to drift into confusion about who actually owns what.

Common questions

How many staff does a small brewery need to start?

Often four to six people can run a small brewery if roles are combined sensibly: brewing and quality, cellar and packaging, sales, and compliance or admin.

Is a formal brewing qualification required to hire a brewer?

Not universally in India yet. Hands-on process discipline and a track record of consistent batches often matter more than a formal qualification at this stage of the industry.

When should a founder stop doing sales and compliance personally?

When either one starts slipping because of the other, that is usually the signal that the business has outgrown one person handling both.

Why is staff retention a bigger issue in brewing than in many small businesses?

Trained brewers and cellar leads are still scarce in India's young craft beer industry, and they are actively recruited between breweries, which makes documented process and deliberate retention worth the investment.
