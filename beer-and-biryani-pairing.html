Which beer goes with biryani

Biryani is not one dish, so it has no single beer. We match the main regional styles to beers that calm the chilli, cut the ghee and leave the saffron alone.

Start with what is on the plate

Ask a table of Indians which beer goes with biryani and most will say whatever lager is coldest. That is a fair answer on a hot afternoon. It is also leaving a lot on the table.

A biryani gives a beer three jobs. Ghee and meat fat coat the mouth, so the beer needs enough carbonation to lift them. Chilli brings heat, so the beer should not make that heat worse. Then there are the delicate aromatics: saffron, kewra, rose water, fried onion, whole spices. A beer that shouts will drown them.

Two rules cover most of this. Bitterness and alcohol both amplify chilli burn, so the hotter the biryani, the lower the IBU and ABV you want. Malt sweetness and fizz do the opposite. They soothe and refresh. Keep those in mind and the regional pairings below make sense.

Hyderabadi biryani: give the chilli some malt

The kacchi style layers raw marinated meat under part-cooked rice and seals the pot for dum. It arrives with green chilli, mint, fried onion and real heat, usually with mirchi ka salan and raita on the side.

This is the biryani that punishes a bitter beer. A 60 IBU IPA turns the chilli from warm to angry. Go for a malt-led lager instead: a Vienna lager, a Märzen or a German dunkel. Their bready, lightly toasty malt rounds off the heat. The moderate bitterness keeps them from tasting sweet next to the salan. A Belgian witbier also works if you want something lighter, because its orange peel and coriander sit nicely beside the mint.

Lucknowi biryani: keep it quiet

The Awadhi or Lucknowi style is the gentle one. Meat and rice are cooked separately and then layered, perfumed with saffron, kewra and sometimes rose. The spice is warm rather than hot.

Here a loud beer is the real risk. A clean Munich helles or a Kölsch-style ale lets the aromatics come through. These beers are fermented cool and clean. A lager typically ferments at around 8 to 13 °C, which keeps the fruity esters low, so there is nothing in the glass competing with the kewra. If you want a little more interest, a lightly hopped German pilsner adds a floral edge without stepping on anything.

Kolkata biryani: sweetness meets sweetness

Kolkata biryani carries its famous potato and often an egg. It is lighter on chilli, with a gentle sweetness and a fragrance that can include kewra and mace.

That mild sweetness likes a beer with some caramel in it. A Vienna lager again, or an English-style bitter with low carbonation and biscuit malt. Avoid very dry, very bitter beers, which make the dish taste flat by comparison.

Malabar and Thalassery: cut through the ghee

The Kerala coast cooks biryani with short-grain kaima or jeerakasala rice, plenty of ghee, fried onions, cashews and raisins. Chicken, mutton, fish and prawn versions are all common. It is rich more than hot.

Richness calls for lift. A saison, with its high carbonation, dry finish and peppery yeast character, scrubs the ghee off the palate and leaves the spices standing. A Belgian blonde does a similar job with a touch more sweetness for the raisins. A crisp pilsner is the safe fallback.

Ambur and Dindigul: pepper wants crisp

The Tamil Nadu styles use seeraga samba rice, a small, fragrant grain. Ambur biryani gets its colour and heat from a dried red chilli paste. Dindigul leans on black pepper and is often eaten with a tangy brinjal side.

Pepper and dried chilli are sharp rather than lingering, so a cold, crisp beer is the best partner: a Dortmunder export, a pale lager or a pilsner kept on the low side of bitterness. A witbier is a good choice if the meal includes the brinjal, since its gentle tartness picks up the tamarind.

How to test this at home

Next time you order biryani for a group, buy two or three contrasting beers instead of a crate of one. Pour small glasses. Take a bite, then a sip. Notice what happens to the chilli and the ghee. Is the heat louder or softer? Does the saffron disappear?

You will find that describing what changed is harder than tasting it. Telling a hop aroma from a yeast ester, or bitterness from roast, is a trained skill. Most people never get past "nice" or "too strong". Our guide to how to taste beer is a good place to begin. These pairings are suggestions, not rules. If your favourite is a cold lager with Hyderabadi dum, nobody is going to stop you.

Common questions

What is the best beer with Hyderabadi biryani?

A malt-led lager such as a Vienna lager, Märzen or dunkel. Low bitterness keeps the chilli from flaring up and the malt softens the heat.

Does IPA go with biryani?

Usually not with a spicy one. Hop bitterness and higher alcohol both make chilli feel hotter. A gentle Kolkata or Lucknowi biryani handles a mild pale ale better than a strong IPA.

Is a regular lager fine with biryani?

Yes. A cold, crisp lager refreshes well. It simply adds less than a beer chosen for the specific style of biryani.

What beer goes with mutton biryani?

A darker, maltier lager such as a dunkel stands up to mutton's richer flavour. For a ghee-heavy Malabar mutton biryani, a saison's carbonation cuts the fat.
