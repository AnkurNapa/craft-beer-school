Brewing certifications, compared

Before picking a certification, it helps to know what each one is actually for. This guide compares the major paths without inventing fees or pass marks that change over time.

3 min read · Updated December 2021 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

On this page

  1. They are not competing for the same job
  2. Start with the job you actually want
  3. Fees and pass requirements change, so check the source
  4. Study time matters more than the certificate itself
  5. These are not mutually exclusive
  6. Where to start if you are still unsure

They are not competing for the same job

Cicerone certification is built around serving and selling beer well, the knowledge a bar manager, server or retailer needs to guide a customer and keep draught lines clean. WSET's beer qualifications sit closer to formal tasting and sensory evaluation, built on the same structured approach WSET is known for in wine. BJCP trains and certifies judges, people who can evaluate a beer against a specific style and explain precisely where it falls short and why, a skill that transfers directly into quality control work at a brewery.

None of them teach you to actually brew at a production scale, control a mash schedule, or troubleshoot a stuck fermentation. That is the job of a brewing-focused course or a formal brewing qualification, and conflating the two is the most common mistake career changers make when picking a first certification, one that often costs both money and months of misdirected study before the right path becomes clear.

Start with the job you actually want

If the goal is working the floor of a good beer bar or building a beverage programme, Cicerone is the closer match. If the goal is developing a trained, portable vocabulary for describing and comparing beer, WSET's structured approach is strong preparation, and it is respected outside the beer world too, since WSET runs the same structured approach across wine, spirits and beer alike. If the goal is judging competitions or developing an exacting internal palate for spotting faults, BJCP is the right target, and our BJCP guide covers what that path actually involves, including how judging experience is usually built up gradually through local competitions over time.

If the goal is standing at a brewhouse controlling a mash, a boil and a fermentation, none of the three replaces hands-on brewing education or supervised time on a real system, covered in our degree versus short course guide, which sets out the two main routes into that kind of hands-on role and what each one actually costs in time.

Fees and pass requirements change, so check the source

Certification fees, exam formats and pass requirements for Cicerone, WSET and BJCP all change over time and are set by the certifying bodies themselves, not by any third party. Rather than quoting numbers here that could be wrong by the time you read this, go to the official Cicerone, WSET or BJCP websites for current fees, exam structure and study materials before committing to a path, since a figure quoted secondhand can easily be a year or two out of date and the exam format itself is occasionally revised without much public notice.

Study time matters more than the certificate itself

A certificate earned by memorising answers without building real tasting skill is close to worthless once you are actually on the job facing a real customer or a real competition entry, whether that job is behind a bar, in a judging panel, at a brewhouse or in a sales role calling on accounts. Treat the exam as a deadline for building a skill you will use for years, not as the end goal in itself, because the certificate on a resume matters far less than the judgement standing behind it in practice.

These are not mutually exclusive

Many serious beer professionals in India and abroad end up holding more than one of these credentials over the course of a long career, because each one covers a genuinely different skill. A Cicerone-certified bar manager who also has a BJCP judging background brings more to a beer programme than either credential would on its own, because the two skills reinforce each other on the floor in ways neither one manages alone.

Where to start if you are still unsure

If you are choosing between all of this and wondering whether to become a brewer at all, our jobs inside a brewery guide and brewery sales career guide are worth reading first, because the right certification depends entirely on which side of the business you actually want to be on, and picking the wrong one first is a common, avoidable delay that a bit of early research easily prevents.

Common questions

Do I need a certification to work in a brewery?

No formal certification is legally required to work in most brewery roles in India, but a recognised credential can help you stand out and can genuinely accelerate the skills a good employer is looking for.

Which certification is respected internationally?

Cicerone, WSET and BJCP are all recognised internationally, though their recognition is strongest in different parts of the industry: hospitality, formal tasting and competition judging respectively.

Can I do these certifications from India?

Yes. All three offer study materials and exams that are accessible from India, either online or through approved local exam centres, though availability varies, so check each body's own site for current options near you.
