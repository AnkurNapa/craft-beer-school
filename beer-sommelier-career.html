A career as a beer sommelier

If you love beer but do not want to stand at a brewhouse mashing grain at six in the morning, this is the career path built around tasting and communicating it instead.

3 min read · Updated May 2023 · Craft Beer School

On this page

  2. Where this work actually exists in India
  3. The skill underneath the title: sensory evaluation
  4. Formal certification and what it signals

What the role actually involves

A beer sommelier evaluates beer quality, builds food pairing menus, trains staff on service and style knowledge, and often represents a brewery or venue in front of customers and media. It sits closer to hospitality and communication than to production, which is exactly why it suits people who love beer but are not drawn to the brewhouse floor.

A good part of the day-to-day work is also curation: choosing which beers go on a tap list or menu, in what order, and how they are described to a customer who may never have tried a style before. That curatorial judgement is built the same way a sommelier's palate is, through repeated, deliberate tasting, not by chance exposure.

Where this work actually exists in India

Craft beer bars, brewpubs, taprooms and larger hospitality groups are the main employers, and the role is often folded into a beverage manager or bar manager position rather than existing as a standalone job title, since the Indian craft beer scene is still young compared to markets with a longer sommelier tradition. Freelance consulting for menus, events and tastings is another route, particularly in cities with an established beer culture.

As the market matures, a dedicated beer sommelier title is becoming more common at larger hospitality groups and hotel chains building out multi-outlet beverage programmes, though it is still the exception rather than the rule. Building the skill now positions you well for that title becoming more standard later.

The skill underneath the title: sensory evaluation

Being able to identify a beer's style, spot a fault like diacetyl or oxidation, and describe flavour precisely and consistently is the actual skill that makes a sommelier valuable, not the title itself. This is learned through structured tasting practice, ideally against reference standards, rather than casual drinking, however much of it you do.

Consistency of vocabulary matters as much as accuracy. If you call a note diacetyl one week and butterscotch the next, without meaning something different each time, your notes stop being useful to anyone trying to act on them, including yourself.

Formal certification and what it signals

International certifications such as the Cicerone programme or WSET's beer qualification exist and are recognised globally, though access and exam availability in India vary and are worth checking directly with the certifying body before committing time and cost to one. A certification is not required to work in the field, but it does signal to employers and clients that your tasting vocabulary has been tested against a standard, not just self-assessed.

Treat a certification as a milestone on top of real tasting practice, not a substitute for it. Someone who has studied for an exam but rarely tastes against reference standards afterwards will fall behind someone who keeps tasting deliberately every week, certificate or not.

Building credibility without a big brand behind you

Running public tastings, writing tasting notes people can trust, and being visibly accurate about faults as well as strengths in a beer builds a reputation faster than any certificate alone. In a small industry, word travels quickly about who actually knows what they are tasting and who is just enthusiastic.

Being willing to say a beer has a fault, even a well-known brewery's beer, is what separates a sommelier people trust from one who only ever says nice things. That honesty is uncomfortable at first, but it is exactly what makes your recommendations worth following.

Food pairing as a specific, learnable skill

Pairing beer with Indian food, matching a crisp lager against fried starters or a malty amber against a rich curry, is a skill that sets a good beer sommelier apart from someone who only knows Western pairing conventions. This is an area where local expertise genuinely beats an imported template, and it is worth developing deliberately rather than copying pairing charts written for a different cuisine.

Spice level and richness matter more than a beer's style name when pairing with Indian food. A high carbonation and clean bitterness can cut through a heavier gravy far better than an imported style chosen only because a menu abroad recommended it, and building your own tested pairings is worth more than borrowing someone else's list.

Common questions

Do I need a certification to work as a beer sommelier in India?

No formal certification is required to work in the field, though a recognised one such as Cicerone or WSET can strengthen your credibility with employers and clients.

Is this a full-time job title in Indian breweries?

Rarely as a standalone title. The work is usually part of a beverage manager or bar manager role, or done as freelance consulting for tastings and menus.

What is the fastest way to build sensory skill?

Structured tasting practice against known style and fault references, ideally guided by a course, rather than casual drinking alone.

Can this career lead into brewing itself?

It can. Strong sensory skills transfer well into quality control roles, and some sommeliers move into brewery quality teams once they understand production.
