Branding a low alcohol or alcohol-free beer next to your core range

More people want a beer without the alcohol. Many Indian craft breweries are thinking about making one. Branding it next to a full-strength range brings its own problems. Here is how to handle them.

3 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Why branding this beer is different

An alcohol-free or low alcohol beer has to do two things at once. It must look like it belongs to your brewery, so drinkers trust it tastes like real beer. It must also be easy to tell apart from your full-strength beers, so nobody picks up the wrong can by mistake. That second point is about safety as much as marketing: a driver, a pregnant guest or someone who does not drink must be able to tell instantly which can is which.

Get this balance wrong in either direction and you have a problem. Too similar and you risk confusion. Too different and the beer looks like an afterthought from another company.

Know the definitions first

"Alcohol-free", "non-alcoholic" and "low alcohol" mean specific things under food and excise rules. The thresholds in India may not match those used in Europe or the UK. FSSAI sets labelling definitions. How a product is classified affects where and how it can be sold. Some states treat these products differently from beer for licensing. Confirm the current definitions and the right label wording with your licensing consultant before naming the product.

This matters for branding because the words on the front of the can are partly fixed by law. Design around them rather than adding them later.

Clear cues on the pack

  • Strength stated large. Put "0.0%" or the actual strength on the front, in large type, not hidden on the back.
  • A distinct colour cue. Many breweries give their alcohol-free line a colour or band that never appears on full-strength beers. Shoppers learn it quickly.
  • Same brand block. Keep the logo and type system so it is still your beer.
  • Different name, related family. A sub-brand name or a clear descriptor beside the beer name helps staff and drinkers.

At the taproom, give it a distinct tap badge and a separate section on the menu. Staff pouring in a rush should never have to read small print to know which tap is which.

The surrogate advertising question

In India, alcohol brands have long used extensions such as soda, water or alcohol-free versions to advertise where beer cannot be advertised. Regulators look closely at extensions that seem to exist only to promote the alcoholic brand. If your alcohol-free beer shares a name with your flagship, think carefully about how you promote it. Take advice before running it in places alcohol brands cannot go. Our guide to alcohol advertising rules in India covers the background.

A real, well-made product sold and promoted on its own merits is on safer ground than a token can made for ads.

Who you are selling to

The drinker for an alcohol-free craft beer is rarely a non-drinker who has never tasted beer. More often it is a regular beer drinker who is driving, working the next morning, cutting down or simply wants a second beer without the alcohol. That shapes the message. Talk about flavour and occasion, not sacrifice. "A proper pale ale for the drive home" beats any language about health.

Avoid health claims entirely. They are a regulatory risk and they invite scrutiny of sugar and calories that you may not want.

The product has to earn the brand

An alcohol-free beer that tastes thin, sweet or worty will damage your core brand, because drinkers will assume the rest of your range has the same care. Making a good one is technically hard. Dealcoholisation needs equipment most brewpubs do not have. The brewing route (special yeasts or restricted fermentation) needs careful recipe design. Low alcohol beers also carry a higher microbial risk, because there is little alcohol to protect them, so pasteurisation or very tight hygiene is usually needed. Our guide to low alcohol craft beer explains the drinker's side.

Brand the beer only once the beer is right. A beautiful can on a disappointing liquid is the fastest way to waste a launch.

A launch checklist

  1. Confirm definitions and label wording with your adviser.
  2. Pick a colour cue and sub-brand name that never appear on full-strength beers.
  3. Put the strength large on the front of the pack and on the tap badge.
  4. Plan promotion that would stand on its own without the flagship's name.
  5. Brew pilot batches until the beer stands up next to your core range.

Common questions

Can an alcohol-free beer use the same name as my flagship?

It can, but it needs a clear descriptor and colour cue. Promotion must stand on its own. Extensions used mainly to advertise an alcoholic brand attract regulatory attention in India.

What does alcohol-free mean legally in India?

FSSAI sets labelling definitions. They may not match other countries. Check the current regulation or ask a licensing consultant before you name the product.

Do alcohol-free beers need pasteurising?

Usually some form of microbial control is needed, because low alcohol gives the beer little natural protection. Pasteurisation or very tight hygiene and cold storage are common approaches.
