The Cicerone certification

The Cicerone certification system has become the standard credential for beer service professionals. Here is what the levels actually test and who genuinely benefits from pursuing it.

3 min read · Updated February 2022 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer

  2. The levels build on each other
  3. How people usually prepare
  4. Where it fits an Indian beer career
  5. It complements, rather than replaces, hands-on brewing

What Cicerone actually tests

Cicerone's certification levels are built around the practical knowledge a beer professional needs on the floor: draught systems and line cleaning, proper storage and serving temperature, food pairing, style knowledge, and enough off-flavour recognition to catch a bad pour before it reaches a customer. It is less about brewing chemistry and more about keeping beer in the condition the brewer intended once it leaves the brewery and travels through distribution, storage and finally a tap or a bottle opener, often across a long, imperfect and occasionally poorly refrigerated supply chain.

This is exactly why it suits bar managers, servers, beverage directors and brewery taproom staff so well. The exam rewards someone who has actually worked with draught systems and served customers under pressure, not just someone who has read a study guide cover to cover without ever pulling a bad pint themselves and having to explain why it went wrong.

The levels build on each other

Cicerone runs a tiered structure, moving from an entry-level certification through more advanced levels that test deeper technical and sensory knowledge, including tasting exams at the higher tiers. Each level has its own study guide and exam format published directly by Cicerone, and the structure has been refined and adjusted over the years as the beer industry itself has changed, so the official site is the accurate source for exactly what each level currently covers, what it costs, and roughly how long candidates typically spend preparing before sitting the exam.

Do not skip a level to save time. Each one is designed to build the knowledge the next one assumes you already have, and attempting the higher tiers without that grounding is a common, entirely avoidable reason candidates fail and have to retake an exam they were not actually ready for yet.

How people usually prepare

Most successful candidates combine structured study of Cicerone's own syllabus with real, hands-on floor experience: actually cleaning a line, actually tasting through style examples, actually handling customer questions under pressure. Reading alone rarely carries someone through the tasting components at the higher levels, because those sections test a trained palate built over months of deliberate practice, not a list of memorised facts recited under exam conditions.

A study group or a mentor who has already passed the level you are attempting is worth more than another round of solo reading, because a real person can push back on a wrong answer immediately, in a way a static textbook simply cannot.

Where it fits an Indian beer career

As India's taproom and brewpub scene has grown, Cicerone knowledge maps directly onto the skills a strong taproom manager or beverage lead actually needs day to day: keeping draught lines clean in Indian heat and humidity, which is a genuinely harder job here than in a cooler climate, storing beer correctly given inconsistent cold chain infrastructure between brewery and outlet, and guiding customers through a style list that is often still unfamiliar to a general Indian drinker used mainly to mainstream, mass-market lager.

A manager who can explain, calmly and specifically, why a particular keg is pouring cloudy or gushing builds far more trust with an owner and a customer alike than one who can only shrug and call the distributor.

It complements, rather than replaces, hands-on brewing

Cicerone will not teach you to run a mash or manage a fermentation, and it is not meant to. If the goal is eventually brewing rather than serving, pair it with genuine production experience, covered in our assistant brewer guide, or with a structured brewing course that puts you in front of a mash tun rather than only a draught line. The two skill sets overlap less than most newcomers to the industry initially expect when they start out.

Common questions

Is Cicerone the same as a sommelier certification for beer?

It is often described that way informally, but Cicerone is built specifically around beer service, storage and draught systems rather than a direct copy of the wine sommelier model.

Do I need bar experience before attempting Cicerone?

It is not a formal requirement, but real floor and draught system experience makes the practical and tasting components far easier to prepare for and eventually pass.

Where do I find current fees and exam dates?

On Cicerone's own official website, since fees, formats and exam locations change over time and are set directly by the certifying body.
