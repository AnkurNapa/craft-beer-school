Storing leftover hops and malt between brew days

Almost every brew day leaves half a pack of hops and a bag of specialty malt behind. Stored badly, they go stale within weeks in Indian heat. Stored well, they will be just as good for the next batch.

4 min read · Updated October 2026 · By Ankur Napa

Written byAnkur NapaCourse Instructor · Master Brewer
Point of contactChatty GirijaQuestions about this guide? WhatsApp her

  1. Leftovers are where the money goes
  2. Hops belong in the freezer
  3. Label everything

  5. Keep the insects out

Leftovers are where the money goes

Hops come in 50 or 100 gram packs. Recipes rarely use a whole pack. Specialty malts are sold by the kilogram while recipes call for 200 grams. So every brewer builds up a shelf of half-used packets. Much of the cost of homebrewing ends up sitting there.

How you store that shelf decides whether the next beer tastes fresh or tired. Heat, oxygen, light and moisture are the four things to keep out. India brings plenty of all of them.

Hops belong in the freezer

Hops lose their bitterness and aroma through oxidation. Heat speeds it up. Cold slows it right down. A domestic freezer at around minus 18 °C is the best place for hops at home.

The other half of the job is keeping air out. After opening a pack, push the air out, fold the top over several times and seal it with tape or a clip, then put it inside a zip bag and squeeze that flat too. A vacuum sealer is better still and pays for itself if you brew often. Oxygen trapped in the pack keeps working even in the freezer, just more slowly.

Stored this way, opened pellets keep good aroma for months. Sealed, unopened packs in the freezer can last a year or more. A half pack left at room temperature in a hot kitchen will taste noticeably flat within weeks.

Label everything

Write the variety, the alpha acid percentage from the pack, the crop year and the date you opened it on every bag. A freezer full of unlabelled green pellets is no help at all. Two pale green bags can be a 4% aroma hop and a 13% bittering hop. Mixing them up changes your bitterness by a factor of three.

Use the oldest open packs first. If a hop has been open for many months, use it for bittering rather than late additions, since bittering depends on alpha acids and aroma loss matters less there.

Malt needs dry and airtight

Malt is dried to low moisture, usually around 4 to 5% for base malts. It wants to pull water back from the air. In the monsoon, an open bag can go from crisp to chewy in days. Chewy malt mills badly and tastes stale.

Store whole malt in airtight food containers, the sort used for rice or atta, somewhere cool and away from the stove and sunlight. Whole grain kept like this lasts for months. Crushed malt goes stale much faster because the inside of the kernel is exposed, so use it within a couple of weeks and buy crushed grain only for a brew you have scheduled.

Specialty malts deserve extra care. Crystal and roasted malts lose their aroma first. These are the malts that give a beer its character. Our guide to brewing an amber ale with crystal malt shows how much a small amount does.

Keep the insects out

Grain beetles and moths love malt. Indian kitchens give them plenty of chances. Airtight containers keep them out of stored grain. If a new sack arrives with signs of insects, a couple of days in the freezer for a small bag is a common household way to kill them. Then sieve out what you can or, if it is badly infested, throw it away.

Keep the storage area clean. Spilt grain on the floor of a cupboard is an open invitation.

Extract and yeast

Liquid malt extract darkens and picks up stale flavours with age and heat. Once opened, keep the tin or pouch sealed in the fridge and use it within a few weeks. Unopened, store it somewhere cool and use the oldest first. Dry malt extract absorbs moisture and sets into a brick, so seal it tightly in a zip bag inside an airtight container.

Dry yeast goes in the fridge, sealed. A half-used sachet is risky; fold it, tape it and use it within days, or just pitch the whole sachet. Our guide to getting the best from malt extract covers how age affects extract beers.

Brew to use what you have

The best storage plan is not to keep things long. Every couple of months, check the shelf and design a beer around what is there. A single malt, single hop beer is a good way to use up a hop and learn exactly what it tastes like at the same time.

Your first step: empty your brewing cupboard onto the table, label every hop pack and move the hops into the freezer, sealed tight.

Common questions

How long do hops last in the freezer?

Sealed packs can last a year or more. Opened packs kept airtight in the freezer keep good aroma for several months.

How should I store malt in India?

In airtight food containers somewhere cool, dark and dry. Whole malt lasts months this way; crushed malt should be used within a couple of weeks.

Can I use old hops?

Older hops are fine for bittering, which depends on alpha acids. Use your freshest hops for late and dry hop additions where aroma matters.
